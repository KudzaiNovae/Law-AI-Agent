Last week I launched OneGov AI, an assistant that answers questions about Zimbabwean law from the actual Acts and Statutory Instruments. The same day, I got a rejection email from the hackathon I originally built it for.

I'll be honest, it stung. I read the email twice, then checked my dashboard and people were already using the thing. So I decided the users mattered more than the rejection and kept going.

The original idea I pitched to the POTRAZ AI for Impact Hackathon was simple: put government information and laws behind one agent that any citizen can ask. It answers instantly with citations, and only escalates to a human officer when something actually needs one, like an application or a dispute. Instead of moving from office to office, or queuing for hours to be told you are in the wrong building, you ask and you know.

One week in: about 600 visitors and over 200 questions answered.

The number one question, by far, is "what are my rights when arrested by the police?" Others that keep coming up: how much notice an employer must give before firing you, what you need for a civil marriage, whether police can impound your car for not paying a spot fine. Someone asked in Shona, "ndezvipi zvinotarisirwa pakuroora zviripamutemo", and got an answer in Shona.

These are not lawyers doing research. They are workers, drivers, couples, people starting businesses. And this is why the project matters to me. When people cannot find the real rules, someone sells them fake ones. Extra requirements that do not exist, fees that were invented at the door. Knowing the law is the only defence, and right now that knowledge is buried in PDFs and gazettes.

Every document on the platform is free to read and download, and it always will be. The site is onegovai.app if you want to try it, and I would love feedback, especially from lawyers, HR people and anyone in government.

A rejection is an opinion, not a verdict.
