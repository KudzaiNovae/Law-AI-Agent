REASONS


CHIVIZHE, J:


This matter was placed before the court as a chamber application for leave to appeal against a judgment rendered by this court under reference LC/H//105/24 on the 12TH March ,2024. The application was opposed. Prior to the hearing, the Applicant indicated to the clerk of court that he was not going to be present on the date of hearing due to a pressing commitment. The matter accordingly proceeded in his absence.

After hearing the submissions from the Respondent Counsel and upon consideration of the record of proceedings, the court issued an order under reference LC/H/1089/25 dated 27th November, 2025 in the following terms:

Accordingly, it is ordered as follows:


  1. The first point in limine, being with merit, be and is hereby upheld.


  1. The application for leave having being filed out of time and for want of an application for condonation, is therefore improperly before the court.

  1. It shall not be necessary to determine the remaining points in limine.


  1. The Applicant shall pay the Respondent’s costs on the ordinary scale with the rider that should he file any further application based on ill-advised grounds punitive costs shall be warranted.

Subsequently, by letter dated 2nd December 2025, the Applicant requested reasons for the court’s decision. The following are the reasons for the order granted on the 27th November, 2025.

BRIEF BACKGROUND


The application for leave to appeal was filed on the 16th October, 2025. In opposing the application for leave the Respondent raised three preliminary points. The first point in limine was that the application for leave to appeal had been filed out of time having been filed outside the twenty-one-day period prescribed by the rules, in the absence of an application for condonation. The application was therefore improperly before the court. The Respondent prayed that the application be struck off.

The second point in limine was that the application in any event was incompetent as this court lacked jurisdiction to entertain a second application for leave to appeal. It was noted that the previous application for leave to appeal filed on 3rd June, 2025 under reference LC/H/479/25 had been struck off the roll with no order as to costs on 25th July, 2025. The Respondent submitted that on the basis of Rule 60(2) of the Supreme Court Rules, as read together with section 92F(3) of the Labour Act, where the Labour Court refuses or strikes off an application for leave to appeal, recourse thereafter lies with the Supreme Court. The Respondent prayer was that the application being improperly before the court had to therefore be struck off the roll with costs.

The third point in limine was that the intended Notice of Appeal to the Supreme Court was incurably defective, as the relief sought by the Applicant was legally incompetent. It was noted that the relief sought by the Labour Officer in the main matter under reference LC/H/233/23 was for confirmation of the Labour Officer’s draft ruling, as provided for by the law applicable at the time. On the basis of this position this court could only grant in relief, either an order confirming the draft ruling or an order refusing to confirm the draft ruling. It was noted that through his intended relief which was couched in part as follows;

“3. The appellant is allowed to lodge an appeal before a different Judge against the Labour Officer’s determination within such timelines as allowed in the Labour Court Rules from the date of this judgment”

The relief sought was clearly legally untenable and unknown to law. The Respondent further noted that this court had in the first application for leave filed under reference LC/H/479/25 released a judgement explaining the reasons for the order striking off that application. In the judgement under reference Clever Sithole vs Minerals Marketing Corporation of Zimbabwe LC/H/335/25 the court had again addressed the issue of an incompetent relief sought. The court had specifically addressed the effect of a Draft Notice of Appeal to the Supreme Court which does not conform to the |Rules of the Supreme Court. The court noted that the effect of such a fatally defective Draft Notice of Appeal was to render the whole application fatally irregular. It was submitted that the application in this case being again premised upon a fatally defective draft Notice of Appeal, the court was urged to strike off the roll the application for leave.

THE APPLICANT’S RESPONSE


The Applicant filed a response through an Answering Affidavit which was filed without leave of court, contrary to the provisions of rule 26 of the Labour Court Rules, 2017. He submitted that the present application was a reinstatement of the previous application for leave to appeal which had been struck off by this Court under reference LC/H/479/25.

He further submitted that the judgment reference LC/H/335/25 striking off that earlier application for leave had invited him to effect procedural corrections, the present application had thus been filed to address these procedural errors. The application had been filed within a reasonable time after receipt of the judgment with reasons. It was his view that the application was therefore properly before the Court.

With regard to the second point in limine he contended that the earlier application for leave was not dismissed on merits. As it was struck off the court still retained jurisdiction to entertain the application which had addressed the procedural defects in the first application for leave.

With regard to the third point in limine, the Applicant submitted that the relief sought was clear and legally sound. He contended that as he intended to set aside a judgment rendered by this Court on 11TH March, 2024 under reference LC/H/233/23, which judgement was based on the now repealed provisions of section 93(5a) of the Labour Act [Chapter 28:01] the draft

Notice of Appeal was therefore competent. The Respondent objection was not properly taken in the circumstances. He urged the Court to dismiss all three points in limine .as they clearly lacked merit.

SUBMISSIONS BY PARTIES


On the date of hearing, the court heard submissions by the Respondent, the Applicant having sought and obtained leave of absence prior to the date of set down. The court after receiving submissions from Respondent Counsel then handed down an ex-tempo ruling upholding the first point in limine, which was that the application had been filed out of time, absent an application for condonation. The application was therefore improperly before the court. It was also the court’s finding that the first point in limine was dispositive of the matter, rendering it unnecessary to adjudicate upon the second and third points in limine.

EVALUATION


The principle that litigants must comply with the rules of court is sacrosanct and fundamental to the administration of justice. Rule 43(1) of the Labour Court Rules, 2017 is very clear. A person wishing to seek leave to appeal the decision of this court must do so within twenty-one days from the date of that decision. It is important to trace the path that this matter has taken in order to fully comprehend and determine the first point in limine as taken. The main judgment that the Applicant intends to appeal against in the Supreme Court was rendered on 11th March 2024 in the application for confirmation of a Labour Officer ruling reference LC/H/233/23. The Applicant initially filed an initial application for condonation for late noting of an application for leave under reference. That application was struck off due to procedural defects. Undeterred the Applicant who was now appearing as a self-actor filed a second application for condonation under refence LC/H/1259/24. The application was granted by the court on 27th May, 2025 and a period of ten (10) days from the date of granting of the order was given for him to file the Application for Leave. The ten (10) days period expired on 10th June, 2025. The Applicant had utilised that condonation by filing an application for leave to appeal under reference LC/H/479/25 on 3 June 2025, which application was subsequently struck off the roll-on 28 July 2025 due to procedural defects.

The application for leave to appeal in this matter was then filed on 16th October, 2025. It was a second application for leave. There was no doubt that the application had been filed, well outside the prescribed time limits, and without a fresh application for condonation for the

late filing. The Applicant’s contention that the application as filed was a “reinstatement” or an extension of the first condonation granted on 27th May, 2025 was legally untenable. The doctrine of functus officio applies to orders of condonation. Once a court grants condonation and the litigant acts upon it by filing the substantive application, that indulgence is fully consumed. The subsequent striking off of that substantive application does not, as a matter of law, resurrect the expired condonation or create a perpetual right to re-file. It is trite that that where an application prosecuted under a granted condonation fails, any new application filed out of time requires its own fresh condonation. The striking off of the first application returned the parties to the status quo ante, but it did not reset or extend the judicial stopwatch. The deadline of 10th June 2025 granted on the 27th May,20215 remained extant. Therefore, the application filed on 16 October 2025 was prima facie late. The applicant had to apply for condonation, showing good cause for the delay and reasonable prospects of success. The facts in the record clearly showed that Applicant had not filed such application. He had offered no explanation, let alone a good one, for the delay from the 25th July, 2025 which is the date when the first application for leave was struck off to 16th October, 2025. It was also patently clear that the judgement he was intending to appeal against having been rendered on 11th March, 2024 on the basis of rule 43(1) the twenty-one days within which the application for leave ought to have been filed expired on the 10th April, 2024. The application for leave had been filed on 16th October, 2025 well outside the prescribed period under the Rules. Consequently, the application was not properly instituted. A court cannot entertain an application that is procedurally stillborn. The court cannot condone a failure to comply with the rules where no application for condonation has been placed before it for consideration.

The court was not persuaded by the Applicant submission that the application for condonation granted on 27th May,2025 following which he had then filed the first application for leave, could be extended to cover the second application for leave and that there was therefore no need for him to seek condonation. The Applicant clearly misconceived the role and purpose of an application for condonation in seeking to comply with provisions of court rules. It needs no emphasis that the Labour Court operates on the basis of prescribed timelines which ought to be complied with. In this case the Appellant had utilised the leave granted to him and filed on application for leave on the 3rd of June, 2025. That application for leave had however been struck off by this court on 28th of July, 2025.

Once the Applicant utilised the condonation granted to him, that indulgence extended had been exhausted. The striking off of the earlier application for leave did not however revive

or extend the original condonation order. Any subsequent application for leave to appeal filed outside the prescribed time limits necessarily required a fresh application for condonation. In the absence of such an application, the application for leave as filed on 16 October 2025 was clearly out of time and thus improperly before the Court.

COSTS


On the issue of costs, counsel for the Respondent submitted that the Respondent had been placed in a difficult position, having been repeatedly dragged to court by the Applicant through what appeared to be baseless and procedurally defective applications. It was further submitted that the Applicant is a sophisticated litigant who appreciates legal processes, yet continues to pursue defective applications despite clear guidance from the court. The Respondent Counsel was therefore praying for costs on the punitive scale as a deterrence to the Applicant.

While the Court was mindful of the need to discourage frivolous and vexatious litigation, it was not persuaded that the application before it, though fundamentally flawed, had been brought in bad faith as to warrant punitive costs at that juncture. The court was also cognisant of the fact that the Applicant had not been asked to pay costs in the previous applications. Costs on the ordinary scale were therefore appropriate to indemnify the Respondent for expenses incurred with a rider that should the Applicant opt to file any other frivolous application punitive costs would be levied against him. The court deemed this as necessary in view of Applicant’s previous conduct. The Applicant, who had demonstrated a clear understanding of legal processes throughout the several applications, needed to be cautioned on the dangers and risk of continuously filing frivolous applications, that it certainly comes with consequences. The court would in such eventuality award costs against him on a punitive scale, as provided for in Rule 39 of the Labour Court Rules, 2017. There was a need for the court as well as the other litigants to be protected from abuse of process. The Applicant was simply being urged to take heed of the court previous call for him to seek professional legal advice before filing any other application in the matter.

It was for the above reasons that the order was issued.
