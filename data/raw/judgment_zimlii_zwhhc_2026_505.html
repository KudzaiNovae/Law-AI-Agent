KWENDA J: The applicants filed separate applications for leave to appeal against both conviction and sentence. This judgement disposes of both applications which are opposed by the state.

The applicants were convicted and sentenced at a joint trial which concluded on the 8th of December 2025. In terms of s 44 of the High Court Act [Chapter 7:06] a person convicted at a criminal trial held by the High Court may appeal to the Supreme Court against conviction and sentence, but requires the leave of a judge to appeal against conviction if the appeal is based on any ground of appeal which involves a question of fact alone or a question of mixed law and fact. The procedure of applying for leave is set out in r 94 of the High Court Rules, 2021.The applicants intended to make oral applications immediately after sentence was passed on the 8th December 2025 in terms of r94 (1)(a) of the High Court rules but I directed them to proceed in terms of r94 (2) and make the applications in writing and file same with the registrar within twelve days of the date of the sentence. I directed that the rules of this court would apply to the service of the applications and filing of subsequent papers.

The Registrar placed the opposed matters before me, in chambers. I invited the parties to appear before me, in court, just in case they had oral submissions to make. At the sitting, the parties agreed that I could exercise the powers given in the rules to grant or refuse the applications on the papers before me, as I found fit.

In an application for leave to appeal, the decision whether to grant leave to appeal depends on the prospects of success. If the prospects are reasonable, the application should be granted; otherwise, it should be refused. In order to succeed, it is not enough for the applicant to make out an arguable case, for there are very few cases which are not arguable in the wide sense of the word. Some authorities in South Africa seemed to regard there being a reasonable prospect of success if the case is arguable. It is clear, however, that what was meant by an arguable argument was that there should be substance in the argument. Where there is substance in an argument, there must ipso facto be a reasonable prospect of success. See S v McGown 1995(2) ZLR 81 (S). An application for leave to appeal against sentence should be treated less rigidly than one to appeal against conviction, because the assessment of sentence is one of the more difficult tasks with which a judicial is faced. S v McGown 1995(2) ZLR 81 (S).

The applicants intend to appeal against both conviction and sentenced.

The background to the applications is as follows: -

The applicants appeared before me and two assessors as accused persons at a joint trial charged with fraud as defined in s136 of the Criminal Law (Codification and Reform) Act [Chapter9:07] (the Criminal Code). The crime involved millions of public funds. The applicants pleaded not guilty and the matter proceeded to trial. At the conclusion of the trial, we found the accused persons guilty as co-perpetrators of the crime charged, as provided for in s196A of the Criminal Law Code.

We sentenced the 1st applicant to imprisonment for 22 years, of which we suspended 3 years for 5 years on condition that the applicant did not commit any offence involving dishonesty during the period of suspension for which upon conviction he is sentenced to imprisonment without the option of a fine. Of the remaining 19 years, we suspended 4 years on condition the applicant paid restitution in the sum of US$2 060 250.60 to the fiscus through the registrar of this court, no later than the 28th February 2026. The amount payable by him was informed by the fact that it became clear during the trial that Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd and its other lawful director and an unlawful director were liable to be charged for the crime, would have participated in the proceeds. We sentenced the 2nd applicant to imprisonment for 17 years, of which we suspended 3 years for 5 years on condition that the applicant did not commit any offence involving dishonesty during the period of suspension for which, upon conviction, he is sentenced to imprisonment without the option of a fine. Of the remaining 14 years, we suspended 2 years on condition he paid restitution to the fiscus in the sum of US$964 064.64 through the Registrar of this court no later than the 28th February 2026. The amount payable by him was informed by what the State proved to have been his share in the proceeds of the crime disbursed to him through the bank account of Millytake Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd.

The State case was that sometime during the year 2021, the Government of Zimbabwe set up a Presidential Goat Pass-On Scheme to alleviate poverty among the less privileged persons across the country’s ten provinces. The target was to buy 500 000 special goat breeds for distribution to households throughout Zimbabwe in the scheme to be administered by the Ministry of Lands, Agriculture, Water, Fisheries and Rural Development [hereinafter referred to as the Ministry]. Pursuant to the mandate, the Ministry published a tender on the 3rd of September 2021 for the supply and delivery of the goats together with a standard bidding template setting out mandatory requirements for eligibility. The tender was open to companies only. The bidding company was required to submit identical fastened copies of the bid (preferably spiral bound) with one marked as the ‘original’ and two others, each marked as a ‘copy’. The name of the company submitting a bid would appear on the face of the bid. The company was required to submit its certificate of incorporation, a list of its directors on form CR 14, a valid ZIMRA tax clearance certificate for the year 2021, a valid NSSA compliance certificate for the same period, a clear statement of the payment terms and state delivery period. The Ministry received bids from Kotrum Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd, Millytake Enterprises (Pvt) Limited, Zvikomborero Farms (Pvt) Ltd and Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming. Kotrum Enterprises Pvt Ltd and Millytake Enterprises failed to meet the minimum requirements leading to their disqualification. The bids submitted by Zvikomborero Farms (Pvt) and Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming sailed through whereupon they were, each, awarded specified portions of the tender.

AS stated above, we found the applicants guilty as co-perpetrators of the offence of fraud as defined in s196A of the Criminal Law Code. Persons charged as co-perpetrators may be convicted as such if the State proves that they committed a crime in association with each other and the State adduced evidence to show that each of them had the requisite mens rea to commit the crime, whether by virtue of having the intention to commit it or the knowledge that it would be committed, or the realisation of a real risk or possibility that a crime of the kind in question would be committed. Where the State has proved that the accused persons the crime in association with each other and that each of them had the requisite mens rea to commit the crime the co-accused persons may be convicted as co-perpetrators, in which event the conduct of the actual perpetrator (even if none of them is identified as the actual perpetrator) shall be deemed also to be the conduct of every co-perpetrator, whether or not the conduct of the co-perpetrator contributed directly in any way to the commission of the crime by the actual perpetrator.

The implications of s196A of the Criminal Law Code is that persons charged as co-perpetrators may be convicted as charged if they committed the crime in association with each other and it is not necessary to prove that each co-accused personally did all the acts constituting the essential element of the crime. Proof of association with the intention to commit a crime or the knowledge or realisation that a crime is being committed suffice. Presence at or in the immediate vicinity of the scene of the crime in circumstances which implicate them directly or indirectly in the commission of that crime shall be indicative (but not, in themselves, necessarily decisive) as a factor tending to prove that the co accused persons accused of committing a crime in association with each other together had the requisite mens rea to commit the crime. Association together in any conduct that is preparatory to the conduct which resulted in the crime for which they are charged shall be indicative (but not, in themselves, necessarily decisive) as a factor tending to prove that the co accused persons accused of committing a crime in association with each other together had the requisite mens rea to commit the crime. Engaging in any criminal behaviour as a team or group prior to the conduct which resulted in the crime for which they are charged shall be indicative (but not, in themselves, necessarily decisive) as a factor tending to prove that the co accused persons accused of committing a crime in association with each other together had the requisite mens rea to commit the crime.

At the end of the trial, it was undeniable that Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming did not exist as company incorporated in terms of the laws of Zimbabwe with the legal capacity to act. The Ministry officials dealt with Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming and accepted its bid as valid after being misled into believing that Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming was lawfully the other name of Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd. This was achieved that by fraudulently submitting documents of incorporation belonging to Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd with the fraudulent bid, namely a certificate of incorporation, a list of directors on form CR 14 and a CR6 all in the name of Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd. Realising that Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd had no documents in its name which they could use fraudulently to give the false impression that the fictitious company had paid taxes, duties and rates for which it may have been liable and the contributions as well as payments due under the National Social Security Authority Act (NSSA) [Chapter17:04], they fraudulently submitted two counterfeit certificates. One was a Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA) clearance certificate belonging to Skywalk (Pvt) Ltd which had been altered. The other was a counterfeit a National Social Security Authority (NSSA) compliance certificate belonging to Skywalk (Pvt) Ltd, which had also been altered. As a result of the misrepresentation, the fraudulent bid submitted by the accused persons in the name of a fictitious company was accepted by the Ministry and treated as valid thereby escaping disqualification.

The fraudulent bid sailed through the verification and adjudication processes before being submitted to the Procurement Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe which endorsed recommendations by the Ministry to award portions as one of the two successful bidders. The confusion created by the misrepresentation resulted in officials at the Ministry and the Procurement Regulatory Authority, officials in the Ministry of Lands referring to Blackdeck Livestock & Poultry Farming by various names such as Blackdeck, Blackdeck Livestock, Blackdeck Livestock & Poultry Farming, Colenso Farm and Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd, interchangeably, during the vetting and adjudication processes and related correspondences. The false impression created by the use of various names created so much confusion in the minds of the government officials that one Precious Isa, the official responsible for procurement at the Ministry drafted a contract in which she made Blackdeck Livestock & Poultry Farming a party genuinely but erroneously believing it had the legal capacity to act and it was lawfully known by that name. Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming, is cited in the contract as a legal persona which could supply 632 001 goats valued at US$87 757 168.00 under the Presidential Goat Pass-On Scheme.

Pursuant to the conclusion and the signing of the contract, ostensibly, reached between it and the non-existent company, the Government of Zimbabwe, paid a total sum of ZWL$1.6 billion (equivalent to US$7 712 197.10 at the prevailing interbank rate) to the fictitious company in expectation of delivery of goats, equivalent in value to the money paid. The money was paid into the CABS bank account of Blackdeck (Pvt) number 1006126120 which had been provided in the contract by the accused persons under the guise of the fictitious Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming. Upon receipt of the money from the Ministry, Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd immediately, transferred huge amounts to Ceder Farming (Pvt)Ltd, Nomistal Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd and Millytake Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd. The rest of the money was disbursed from Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd’s bank account.

Millytake Enterprises (Pvt)Ltd is a company controlled by the 2nd accused person as its director. It received a total sum of ZWL$200 000 000.00 (Two Hundred Million United States Dollars) in its CABS bank account number 1129742431.

It was common cause at the trial that Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd’s bank account was overdrawn prior to the disbursement of funds by the Ministry into its bank account. It was also common cause that in no time all the money had been disbursed and the account had reverted to its overdrawn status. It was also common cause, at the trial, that all the companies which, in turn, received money initially disbursed to the fictitious company by the Ministry, unlawfully, traded the same on the foreign exchange black-market in exchange for United States dollars. It was also common cause that the amount of foreign Currency which was raised was neither banked nor disclosed. In terms of the Bank Use Promotion Act [Chapter 24:24] the companies were, in the year 2021, required, in the absence of good cause for not doing so, deposit the all foreign currency at their disposal in excess of two hundred dollars, into an account with a financial institution no later than the close of normal business hours on the day following their receipt of the money. It was common cause that in terms of the statement of mandatory requirements in Part 2 of the Standard Bidding Document, under the heading ‘CORRUPT PRACTICES’, the Ministry, the bidders and contractors were required to adhere to the highest standard of ethics during the procurement and execution of the contract. The trading the money received from the Government on the black market for foreign currency was not only unauthorised by the Government which pegged prices at the official rate but was also unlawful and, for that reason alone, unethical. As regards the sentence, I note the instructive sentiments McGown case, supra, that an

application for leave to appeal against sentence should be treated less rigidly than one to appeal against conviction, because the assessment of sentence is one of the more difficult tasks with which a judicial is faced. The McGown case was decided in 1995, that is eighteen years before the promulgation of the Criminal Procedure (Sentencing Guidelines) Regulations, 2023, Statutory Instrument 146 of 2023. The sentencing guidelines have made sentencing easier for judicial officers who follow them to the letter.

The law is that a court of appeal will only interfere with the sentencing discretion of a lower court if it considers that a substantial miscarriage of justice occurred. It will only do so if the lower court committed an irregularity or a misdirection committed by the lower court. If there has been no misdirection, the appeal court will not be at large to interfere with the sentencing discretion of the trial court. In any event there can only be a substantial miscarriage of justice if the sentence is disturbingly inappropriate or so severe as to induce a sense of shock. See S v Sidat 1997 (1) ZLR 487.

Incidences of irregularity or misdirection would therefore only arise if the trial court demonstrably failed or neglected to follow the sentencing guidelines. Occurrences of inappropriate sentences or sentences so excessive as to induce a sense of shock should now be a rare occurrence in light of the binding nature of sentencing guidelines, presumptive penalties, the requirement to state sentencing trends and the rigorous mandatory considerations which inform the exercise of sentencing discretion where the sentencing judicial officers departs from the presumptive penalty.

The guidelines were promulgated in the year 2023, pursuant to the provisions of s334A of the Criminal Procedure and Evidence Act [Chapter 9:07], which empowers the Judicial Service Commission, from time to time, to convene a judicial conference for purposes of formulating sentencing guidelines and in particular, provide for

(a) inquiries and investigations to be conducted by courts prior to sentencing offenders;

(b) the factors to be considered by courts when sentencing offenders;

(c) forms of punishment that may be imposed as alternatives to custodial sentences;

(d) principles and criteria which will assist in promoting consistency in sentencing and the equitable.

In terms of s 334A (1) of the Criminal Procedure and Evidence Act further provides that the sentencing guidelines shall be in the form of a table of presumptive penalties supplemented by additional guidelines addressing such of the factors as the judicial conference considers relevant.

The guidelines apply to all criminal proceedings in all courts that conduct trials at first instance. With the objective of fostering public confidence in the criminal justice system by promoting consistency and transformative justice in sentencing and eliminating unwarranted disparities in the punishment of offenders. The guidelines are on all judicial officers. Where they provide for a presumptive penalty, the courts shall pay due regard to the applicable sentencing guidelines when sentencing offenders. A “presumptive penalty” means a penalty expressed as a specific amount of a fine or a specific period of imprisonment or both that is midway between an augmented penalty which may be imposed in aggravating circumstances and a diminished penalty which may be imposed in mitigating circumstances. Where a sentencing court departs from a prescribed presumptive penalty as provided for in these guidelines it shall give reasons for that departure.

The Sentencing Guidelines, therefore, provides for two presumptive penalties with respect to every crime. In the case of fraud, the considerations are as follows. A fraud is deemed to have been committed in aggravating circumstances and the presumptive penalty of imprisonment for twenty years is prescribed where one or more of the following situations obtain: -

  1. the items, funds or property involved were of considerable value.

  2. the funds or the items involved were not recovered.

  3. the offender was in a position of trust.

  4. the offender was a holder of a public office.

  5. the offence involved public funds.

A diminished punishment is prescribed of the fraud was committed in mitigating circumstances. The mitigating circumstances are that the potential and actual prejudice was insignificant or that the offender voluntarily compensated the victim. In that case the presumptive penalty is prescribed as imprisonment for 6 months.

The potential prejudice was in the region of US$26 000 000.00 (Twenty-six million United States Dollars), being 30% deposit of out of the contract price of US$87 757 168.00 which the Government had initially agreed to pay under the Presidential Goat Pass-On Scheme Due to financial constrains Treasury only managed. sum of ZWL$1.6 billion (equivalent to US$7 712 197.10 to the fictitious company. The funds involved were, therefore, of considerable value. The bulk of the money was not recovered.

The actual advance payment made to Blackdeck (Pvt) Ltd was equivalent to USD 7 712 197.00. At the average price of USD 90 .00, the committee had expected to find 85 000 goats. There was shortfall of 28 612 goats (89.6 %). At that stage Blackdeck Livestock and Poultry Farming had supplied 4 208 goats valued at USD331 445 using the addendum contract price giving an outstanding balance of USD7.38 million in terms of goats to be supplied. It is common cause that the fraud committed by the accused persons involved public funds. All the money paid by Treasury constituted proceeds of crime because it was disbursed pursuant to a fraud. The goats supplied 4 208 goats valued at USD331 445 constitute money recovered.

Both applicants agreed that the applicable presumptive penalty was that of 20 years

because the fraud was committed in aggravating circumstances. The court religiously followed the sentencing guidelines. If the court erred at all it would have erred on the side of leniency but the State has accepted the sentence.
