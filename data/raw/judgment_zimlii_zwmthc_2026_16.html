SIZIBA J: This is a court application for an order setting aside the appointment of the third respondent as Village Head Chaibva and the reinstatement of the applicant as Village Head Chaibva. The order sought by the applicant is couched as follows:

  1. The 1st respondent's decision to remove the applicant and appointment of the 3rd respondent as Village Head Chaibva be and is hereby set aside.

  2. The applicant be and is hereby reinstated as Village Head Chaibva.

  3. The Respondents to pay costs of this application on attorney client scale.

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

The dispute in this matter revolves around the question of who is the rightful Village Head Chaibva under Chief Nyashanu in Buhera District of Manicaland Province in Zimbabwe. On 21 January 2021, the applicant was appointed Village Head Chaibva by the first respondent. He alleges that this was after he was recommended to that office by the Chaibva family, Headman Nemhare, Chief Nyashanu and the District Administrator. It is common cause that his appointment was challenged through the Provincial Assembly of Chiefs in Manicaland Province. A committee which was chaired by Chief Hata looked into the matter. He alleges that Chief Hata told him that he was no longer the Village Head at Chaibva Village and that the first respondent had appointed the third respondent as Village Head Chaibva. When he visited the relevant offices in Harare to register his complaint over this issue, he was then furnished with a letter which was signed by the second respondent on first respondent’s behalf confirming that the first respondent had removed him from his office and appointed the third respondent. The letter in question was dated 26 July 2023. Aggrieved by this decision, the applicant filed this application before this court on 21 January 2024 contending that the first respondent acted outside the law as he had removed him without consultation. He further submitted that the third respondent was appointed Village Head Chaibva when he does not qualify to be such as he is not part of the Chaibva family tree.

This application was opposed by all the respondents. It is important to note at this juncture that the opposing affidavit for the first and second respondents was deposed to by the Minister of Local Government and Public Works (Hereinafter referred to as the Minister) without explaining why the first and second respondents were unable to depose to affidavits for themselves.

The third respondent raised two points in limine in his opposing affidavit. He stated that there was no founding affidavit before the court because applicant’s affidavit was wrongly dated. His other point in limine was that this court had no jurisdiction to deal with this matter as the dispute was one that ought to be resolved by the President in terms of s 283 of the Constitution of Zimbabwe. He therefore sought the dismissal of the applicant’s application.

On the merits, the third respondent submitted that the facts presented to the court by the applicant were not true. He averred that the applicant was never the rightful Village Head as he is not even part of the Chaibva family. He contended that the applicant misrepresented facts to the court by producing the wrong family tree which does not enlist the last village head who is a brother to the third respondent. Third respondent further averred that the removal of the applicant was not spontaneous but rather it was after a series of inquiries which were held to determine who the successor to the outgoing Village Head was. It was then concluded that the third respondent was the rightful Village Head.

In response to the first and second respondents’ opposing affidavit, the applicant submitted that there was a strict procedural bar against the first and second respondents in that they were barred by operation of law from taking active part in the proceedings as they had filed their opposition after the expiry of the stipulated time. They had not sought condonation and upliftment of the bar in terms of the rules. Furthermore, the opposing affidavit for these respondents was deposed to by a non-party, the Minister, whose office is distinct from that of the Permanent Secretary, therefore, in that regard, they remained barred. In regard to the opposing affidavit of the third respondent, the applicant submitted that the third respondent failed to address critical facts of his prior, legitimate and documented appointment as Village Head Chaibva.

SUBMISSIONS BEFORE THIS COURT

The applicant in his submissions before this court insisted that the Minister was not properly before the court because he was never cited as a party to the proceedings. This therefore resulted in the cited first and second respondents being barred and any submissions made by the Minister would be a nullity. The applicant also made a submission that since the third respondent did not rescind the default judgment that had been granted against it, it was not properly before the court. In response, counsel for the first and second respondents submitted that that the Minister clearly stated in the opposing affidavit that he had been authorized to depose to the facts because he had knowledge to the facts. He submitted that there was no issue in him deposing to the affidavit on behalf of the first and second respondents. In motivating this position, counsel relied on the case of Mapendere & Ors v Minister of Justice Legal & Parliamentary Affairs & Others HH420/17 wherein it was held that a party can depose to an affidavit on behalf of another party as long as they have authority and knowledge to depose to such facts.

The third respondent on the other hand insisted on the point of law that this court lacks jurisdiction to determine this application. In motivating this point, counsel for the third respondent submitted that s 283 of the Constitution is written in peremptory terms where it provides that disputes arising from the appointment and removal of traditional leaders must be resolved by the President. He further relied on the case of Muchinjiko v Minister of Local Government and Public Works and Others HH383/26. As regards to the point of material disputes of fact, counsel stated that there are disputes in regard to the family tree that was proffered by the applicant and such can be better determined by viva voce evidence.

APPLICATION OF THE LAW TO THE FACTS

The applicant’s point in limine that the first and second respondents are barred because of their failure to file proper opposing affidavits has merit. The Minister’s opposing affidavit that was filed on behalf of these respondents falls far short of the requirements of the law. The Minister did not explain why these respondents could not file opposing affidavits on their own. He also failed to explain how he had been authorized to depose to an opposing affidavit on behalf of these litigants. The reliance by counsel for these respondents on the case of Mapendere and Others v Minister of Justice Legal and Parliamentary Affairs and Others HH 420/17 will not serve these respondents from this automatic bar. A much stricter approach has been adopted in this jurisdiction on this aspect in recent years. See Sandawana Mines (Private) Limited v The Mining Commissioner -Midlands Province N. O and Others HH 539/23. The result is that the purported opposing affidavit for the first and second respondents is a nullity and those respondents are barred. It is no longer necessary for this court to determine the other point in limine relating to the failure by the first and second respondents to file their opposing papers timeously because they are no longer before the court.

The submission by applicant’s counsel that the third respondent is not properly before the court as it did not rescind the default judgment that was rescinded by this court at the instance of the Minister is rather strange. The papers before this court show that the default judgment that had been granted against all the respondents in this matter was rescinded by this court on 30 January 2026 under case number HCMTC41/26. The applicant in that matter was the Minister. If a judgment is rescinded by the court at the instance of any party, such rescission automatically means that there is no longer a judgment against any party in the case in question. There is no need for each of those parties to apply to rescind a judgment that is no longer extant. A judgment can only be rescinded once. Applicant’s submission that the rescinded judgment is operative as against the third respondent is misplaced and without merit at law.

The third respondent’s point in limine that the applicant’s founding affidavit is defective because of a wrong date was not elaborated and it is without merit. That affidavit was properly signed and commissioned and hence the application is valid.

The third respondent’s point in limine that this court lacks jurisdiction to resolve customary succession disputes or disputes concerning the appointment of traditional leaders has merit. In terms of section 283 of the Constitution of Zimbabwe, such disputes must be resolved by the President of this Republic. This position is in line with the now trite position that this court’s jurisdiction will be exercised only for review purposes. See Marange v Marange and Others SC 1/21, Rutsade v Wedzerai and Others SC 45/22, Kamuchenje and Others v Minister of Local Government and Public Works and Others HH 443/24 and Mutasa v Mutasa and Others HMTJ 8/25. The belated attempt by applicant’s counsel to try and classify his application as an application for review proved very futile as it was not such right from the onset. The applicant’s founding affidavit and even the face of the application itself shows that it is not an application for review. That being the case, nothing triggers this court’s review jurisdiction and hence the application is not properly before this court. This finding renders it unnecessary for this court to determine any other outstanding issues. The costs shall follow the successful party. In the result it is ordered as follows:

  1. The applicant’s point in limine that the first and second respondents are barred is hereby upheld.

  2. The applicant’s point in limine that the third respondent is not properly before the court for failure to rescind a default judgment that had been entered against it is dismissed for lack of merit.

  3. The third respondent’s point in limine that applicant’s founding affidavit was not properly signed is dismissed for lack of merit.

  4. The third respondent’s point in limine that this court lacks jurisdiction to resolve disputes concerning traditional leaders and grant the relief sought by the applicant is hereby upheld and as a result, the matter is hereby struck off the roll with costs.
