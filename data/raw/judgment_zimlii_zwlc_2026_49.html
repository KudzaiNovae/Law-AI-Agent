IN THE LABOUR COURT OF ZIMBABWE JUDGMENT NO. LC/H/40/26

HELD AT HARARE CASE NO. LC/H/1288/25

IN THE MATTER BETWEEN:

LIVION KALIMBA APPLICANT

Versus

NATIONAL FOODS (LTD) RESPONDENT

5TH FEBRUARY 2026 and 6TH FEBRUARY 2026.

BEFORE THE HONOURABLE GONESI J

For the Applicant: T.G Kuchenga with C Mashura .

For the Respondent: A.K Maguchu.



GONESI J: This is an application for review where the applicant raises the following grounds of review:

  1. “GROUNDS FOR REVIEW

The disciplinary proceedings were held in a manner which extended undue favour to the Complainant. Throughout the proceedings, the hearing chairperson showed bias against the Appellant and favour towards the Complainant’s cause.

The bias was so apparent in that-:

a. The preliminary point taken by Appellant that the disciplinary committee was not properly constituted was dismissed without giving it due regard. The chairperson was dismissive in his approach of it.

b. Throughout the hearing, the chairperson would interject to assist the Complainant or complainant witnesses in answering questions.

c. Despite protestations, the chairperson allowed Complainant to produce new evidence previously not made available to the Appellant, where Complainant sought to produce evidence, the chairperson would review it first, and deny Appellant the opportunity to see it if he considered that it was not favourable to the Complainant. For example, the statements signed at the police station.

d. Even after denying production of some of the evidence, the chairperson would go on to request that the same evidence be produced at the next hearing.

e. Despite objections, he allowed Mr Maguchu to represent the Complainant without making a determination on the objection.”



Applicant’s prayer was couched as follows:

“WHEREFORE, Applicant prays:

a. That the instant application succeeds.

b. That the decision of the Disciplinary Committee, National Foods dated 9 September 2025 be and is hereby set aside.

c. That the matter be and is hereby remitted to a different Disciplinary Committee for a determination de novo.

d. Respondent pays costs of suit on an attorney-client scale.



  1. FACTUAL CONSPECTUS

    1. Applicant was charged under section 19.8.4 of the National Foods Limited Code of Conduct-Unsatisfactory work performance: Substantial neglect of duties and the allegations upon which the charge was based were two fold. The first one related to failure to execute duties related to maize truck offloading. It was alleged that Applicant signed off six maize trucks that records indicate were not offloaded.

    2. It was further stated that as a control room operator his duties were to ensure that each raw material truck offloads at the correct intake point and assigned to the appropriate destination in the Auto mill system. It was further alleged that the alleged failure to carry out these responsibilities resulted in discrepancies in maize stock management.

    3. The second allegation was that Applicant delayed processing of weighbridge documentation. It was alleged that he signed weighbridge tickets on 19 May 2025 for two trucks which had offloaded on 9 May 2025 which led to inaccurate stock figures during the re-count stocktake of 10 May 2025.

    4. The applicant was convicted and a penalty of dismissal was meted and as a result he filed an appeal with the appeals committee.

    5. At the appeals committee hearing the applicant alleged that the committee lacked the jurisdiction to determine the appeal ,and the committee finding merit in the submissions upheld the preliminary point.

Irked by the decision of the appeals committee to decline jurisdiction, applicant has approached this court with an application for review of the proceedings of the disciplinary committee .



3.PROCEEDINGS BEFORE THIS COURT

3.1. At the commencement of the proceedings I asked the parties to address the court on whether or not the present application was properly before the court.

3.2. Counsel for the applicant maintained that the present application was properly before the court considering the fact that the it was undesirable to file piecemeal applications before the court and the fact that the code of conduct in use provided for the exhaustion of internal remedies and as such applicant was bound to first of all make use of the domestic remedies at his disposal before approaching the labour court, so argued counsel.

Counsel further urged the court to condone the infraction by the applicant in terms of rule 32.

3.3. Per contra, counsel for the respondent submitted that the application was improperly before the court regard being had to the provisions of the rules of the Labour court.

3.4. It was the contention by counsel for the applicant that the application was being made in terms of rule 19 and not rule 20 of this honourable court’s rules.



3.5. The court was not persuaded by the submissions from applicants’ counsel. Whether an application for review was made in terms of rule 19 or rule 20 the question that is begging for an answer is has the application been filed within 21 days as per the rules? In the case before the court the answer is in the negative. Counsel tried to justify the approach by arguing that a review application against the proceedings of the disciplinary committee could not be brought to the labour court in time as applicant was mandated to exhaust internal remedies first. Can this not be taken as acquiescence ?

3.6. Section 89 of the Labour Court Act [Chapter 28:01] provides that,

“89 Functions, powers and jurisdiction of Labour Court

  1. The Labour Court shall exercise the following functions—

(d1) exercise the same powers of review as would be exercisable by the High Court in respect of labour matters...”



Rule 20 of the Labour Court Rules, 2017 provides that,

“Reviews

20. (1) A person wishing to seek review of proceedings referred to in terms of the Act shall, within twenty-one days from the date when the proceedings are concluded, do the following...” (my emphasis)



    1. The provision referred to above imposes a mandatory requirement that any application for review must be instituted within twenty-one (21) days from the date on which the proceedings sought to be impugned were concluded. This statutory timeframe is not merely directory or discretionary it is peremptory in nature and must be strictly complied with.



    1. It is not in dispute that the proceedings before the Disciplinary Authority were finalised on the 9th September 2025. The argument by applicant’s counsel that the proceedings before the Disciplinary Authority and the Appeals Officer were a continuous process is unfounded at law. The Disciplinary Authority made a determination of the matter on the merits thereby marking the close of proceedings.



In the case of Hwende & 2 Ors v The Speaker of the National Assembly & 4 Ors; Timvous v The Speaker of the National Assembly & 4 Ors HH 633/20, it was stated that,

“A court/judge becomes functus officio when he/she has decided a dispute on the merits. See Herbstein & van Winsen The Practice of the High Courts of South Africa 5 ed, Vol 1 at pp 909 and 917 and the cases cited thereat.

At p 909

“The Court has refused to allow a witness to be recalled where the issue of liability had already been decided, rendering the court functus officio.”



and p 917

“Once a court has made an order disposing of the matters in issue, the court becomes functus officio and may not make further orders not sought in the papers set out and defining the lis before the court, unless the parties agree otherwise.”



    1. In casu, the Disciplinary Authority concluded the proceedings after making a determination on the merits. Consequently, the present application has been instituted outside the prescribed time limits. Notably, there is no application for condonation before this Honourable Court to regularise the delay, rendering the application procedurally defective.

    2. There was an attempt by counsel to apply for condonation by citing that this court is empowered to grant a departure from its rules in terms of rule 32.It is the finding of this court that granting such an application is not in the interests of justice especially considering that such an application is not there for the mere asking, a litigant must put himself into the confidence of the court by baring it all, a factor which is absent from the application that was made over the bar.



    1. In the case of Mtetwa E v Mtetwa H & 5 Ors HH-632-25 it was stated that,



“The court has also pronounced on the consequences of non-compliance with peremptory provisions of the rules as held in Chirosva Minerals (Pvt) Ltd v Minister of Mines and Ors 2011 (2) ZLR 274, wherein it was held that the disregard of a peremptory provision in a statute is fatal to the validity of the proceedings effected․ Thus, the non-compliance with the peremptory provisions of the rules or statute would render the pleading fatally defective and therefore, a nullity.



McNally JA in Dalny Mine v Banda stated that,



“As a general rule, it seems to be undesirable that labour relations matters should be decided on the basis of procedural irregularities. By this I do not mean that such irregularities should be ignored. I mean such irregularities should be put right”



    1. In casu, the failure by the applicant to apply for condonation before filing the application for review out of time is a fatal irregularity that can only be put right by an order striking off the matter from the roll to enable the applicant to put their house in order and approach the court again after attending to the defects raised.

DISPOSITION

The application for review be and is hereby struck off the roll.

There shall be no order as to costs.



MASHURA MOYO ATTORNEYS applicants’ Legal Practitioners.

MAGUCHU & MUCHADA respondent’s Legal Practitioners.
