Court application for review

R. Zimudzi, for the applicant

M. B. Hungwe, for the 6th respondent


DUBE-BANDA J:


  1. This is an application for review filed in terms of s 27 of the High Cour Act [Chapter 7:06] as read with r 59 and r 62 of the High Court Rules, 2021. The applicants describe themselves as the direct descendants of what they call the founding patriarch of the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy, Chief Chamagwenjere. The first respondent is the President of Zimbabwe, “the President”, cited as in his capacity as the constitutional authority with the power to appoint, suspend, and remove traditional leaders in terms of the Constitution of Zimbabwe Amendment (No. 20) 2013 as read with the Traditional Leaders Act [Chapter 29:17] “the Act”. The second respondent is the Minister of Local Government & Public Works “the Minister”, he is responsible for the administration of traditional leadership affairs, including making recommendations to the President concerning the appointment, suspension, and removal of traditional leaders in terms of the Act. The third respondent is the Chairperson of the Masvingo Provincial Assembly of Chiefs, the institution that offers guidance on matters of custom and tradition in the appointment of traditional leaders and assists in the resolution of disputes about traditional leadership. The fourth respondent is the District Development Coordinator for Zaka District; he is responsible, at district level, for coordinating and supervising chieftainship succession meetings. The fifth respondent is the Provincial Development Coordinator for Masvingo Province, the administrative supervisor of all district development coordinators in Masvingo Province. The sixth respondent is Owen Maguta who was declared and installed as




the substantive Chief Nyakunhuwa, whose installation the applicants are contesting in this application.


  1. The applicants seek an order reviewing and setting aside the decision of the President appointing the sixth respondent as Chief Nyakunhuma. The basis upon which the applicants bring this application is set out in the application and founding affidavit as follows:


    1. That the respondents failed to consider and apply the relevant customary law principles, customs, and traditions of the Nyakunhuwa community in the appointment of the sixth respondent as Chief Nyakunhuwa.

    2. That the respondents failed to afford the applicants the opportunity to be heard or make representations prior to the decision being made.

    3. That the respondents acted unfairly and in breach of administrative justice by failing to acknowledge, process, or determine the appeal lawfully filed by the applicants with the provincial Assembly of Chiefs prior to the appointment of the sixth respondent.

    4. That the respondents acted in breach of a process which was vitiated by gross irregularities and lack of transparency in the appointing process.


  1. In this review application filed in terms of the High Cour Act [Chapter 7:06] as read with High Court Rules, 2021 as stated above, the applicants seek an order couched in the following terms:


    1. The application be and is hereby granted.

    2. The decision of the first respondent appointing the 6th respondent, Onwell Maguta, as Chief Nyakunhuwa be and is hereby set aside.

    3. The recommendation by the second respondent to the first respondent which formed the basis of the aforesaid decision, be and is hereby set aside.

    4. The second to fifth respondents be and are hereby ordered to reconvene, within 30

days of this order, a lawful, inclusive, and procedurally fair succession process for the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy, taking into account the applicable customary principles of succession of Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy.

    1. The respondents, jointly and severally, the one paying the other to be absolved, shall bear the costs of this application.




  1. The application is opposed by the Minister and the sixth respondent. The first, third, fourth and fifth respondents have not filed opposing papers, and I take it they have decided to abide by the decision of the court.


Background facts


  1. This application will be better understood from the following background. The first applicant who deposed to the founding affidavit avers that this application arises from a deeply contested appointment of the sixth respondent to the position of Chief Nyakunhuwa. He contends that he and the other applicants are direct descendants of the founding patriarch of the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy, Chief Chamagwenjere, whose lineage is said to have sustained and preserved the cultural, historical, and customary legacy of the Nyakunhuwa Clan. It is contended that the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy has for generations been governed by a rotational system of succession among the five established royal houses: Mutsvangwa; Mutonhori; Mututuvari; Murembwa; and Machingambi. It is said that the sixth respondent is not a descendant of any of the royal houses; he is alleged to be a descendant of one Baramanza who is not related to Chamagwenjere who established the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy. It is said that the only families who are entitled to ascend to the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy are the above-named royal houses. It is contended that the sixth respondent’s appointment to the position of Chief Nyakunhuwa was unlawful and procedurally irregular, in that it disregarded and violated the clan’s succession principles and contravened the law.


  1. The applicants further contended that Jerera Machingambi, then sitting Chief Nyakunhuwa, died in 2015. After his death, his son, Courage Machingambi, is said to have taken the reins on a temporary basis, pending appointment and installation of a substantive chief. It is stated that on 27 April 2018, a preliminary succession meeting was convened, wherein a point of order was taken to the effect that the sixth respondent was of the Baramanza Clan and was not a legitimate descendant of the Chieftaincy. It is averred that a further meeting was held on 4 September 2018, where it is alleged that the Baramanza family members were ordered to leave the meeting. It is stated that the minutes of this meeting are not authentic, in that they were manipulated. It is further stated that a meeting was held on 4 December 2020, wherein the




applicants were neither invited nor their views sought. It is averred that at this meeting, the sixth respondent was declared the new Chief Nyakunhuwa. It is contended that in December 2022 communication was received that the sixth respondent has been appointed Chief Nyakunhuwa. It is against this background that the applicants filed this application seeking the order stated above.


Preliminary objections


  1. Other than resisting the relief sought on the merits, the sixth respondent, in his opposing papers, took several points in limine which were the subject of argument in this matter. The sixth respondent raised the following points in limine; viz, that this court has no jurisdiction to hear this matter; that the grounds of review are invalid; that there are material disputes of fact incapable of resolution in motion proceedings; and that the relief sought is incompetent. I mention for completeness that the Minister did not participate in the argument on points in limine, however, his opposing papers are before court and cannot be ignored in determining these objections. I now consider the preliminary objections.


  1. At the commencement of the hearing, Mr Hungwe counsel for the sixth respondent abandoned the point in limine on jurisdiction. No further reference shall be made regarding this point in limine in this judgment.


Material disputes of fact


  1. The sixth respondent contends that there are material disputes of fact which cannot be resolved in motion proceedings. It is argued that this court will have no ready answer regarding the Nyakunhuwa genealogy because the parties have attached different family trees, and each party alleges that its family tree is the correct one. In addition, the applicants attached two family trees. Further, it is contended that the authenticity of the documents produced by the applicants as family trees are a fabrication because they were not produced in the meetings held to resolve the appointment of the successor to the chieftaincy. In addition, it is argued that applicants are contesting the minutes of the meetings, while the sixth respondent regards them as correct. The sixth respondent argued that the applicants ought to have realized that this court




would be unable to resolve this matter on the papers, and therefore this preliminary point must be upheld and the application dismissed.


  1. Per contra, the applicants argue that the contention that the matter is riddled with material disputes of fact which cannot be resolved in motion proceedings is more theoretical than real. It is argued that the central issue before the court is not about the contested versions of custom, genealogy or factual minutes requiring oral evidence. It is contended that the issue for resolution is whether there was a failure to consider and apply the relevant customary law principles, customs, and traditions of the Nyakunhuwa community, and whether appeals lodged by the applicants were entertained, and whether they were afforded an opportunity to be heard or make representations prior to the contested decision being made. It is further argued that even if there are factual disagreements, they are not material disputes of fact as envisaged in case law. It is contended that the root of the matter turns on the lawfulness of the process, and the alleged dispute of fact does not deal with that central issue. It is argued that even if the court finds that there are material disputes of fact, it must take a robust approach and resolve the dispute on the papers. The applicant sought that this preliminary point be dismissed.


  1. It is important to restate the basic principles applicable in the resolution of cases in motion proceedings. Motion proceedings are about the resolution of legal issues based on common cause facts. The courts have accepted, though, that in certain instances a robust and common-sense approach can lead to a resolution of the matter on the papers without causing an injustice to either party. In Muzanenhamo v OIC, Law & Order & Ors 2013 (2) ZLR 604 (S) at 608 A-F, the court said:


“As a general rule in motion proceedings, the courts are enjoined to take a robust and common sense approach to disputes of fact and to resolve the issues at hand despite the apparent conflict. The prime consideration is the possibility of deciding the matter on the papers without causing injustice to either party.


The first enquiry is to ascertain whether or not there is a real dispute of fact. As was observed by Makarau JP (as she then was) in Supa Plant Investments (Pvt) Ltd v Chidavaenzi 2009 (2) ZLR 132 (H) at 136F-G:




‘A material dispute of facts arises when material facts alleged by the applicant are disputed and traversed by the respondent in such a manner as to leave the court with no ready answer to the dispute between the parties in the absence of further evidence.’


In this regard, the mere allegation of a possible dispute of fact is not conclusive of its existence. See Room Hire Co. (Pty) Ltd v Jeppe Street Mansions (Pty) Ltd 1949 (3) SA 1155 (T) at 1163; Checkers Motors (Pvt) Ltd v Karoi Farmtech (Pvt) Ltd S-146-86; Boka Enterprises v Joowalay & Another 1988 (1) ZLR 107

(S) at 114B-C; Kingstons Ltd v L.D. Ineson(Pvt) Ltd 2006 (1) ZLR 451 (S) at 456C-D and 458D-E. The respondent’s defence must be set out in clear and cogent detail. A bare denial of the applicant’s material averments does not suffice. The opposing papers must show a bona fide dispute of fact incapable of resolution without viva voce evidence having been heard. See the Room Hire Co. case, supra, at 1165, cited with approval in Vittareal Flats (Pvt) Ltd v Undenge & Others 2005 (2) ZLR 176 (H) at 180C-D; Van Niekerk v Van Niekerk & Others 1999 (1) ZLR 421 (S) at 428F-G”


  1. The first enquiry is to ascertain whether there is a real dispute of fact. The answer to this question requires closer scrutiny of the applicant’s founding affidavit and grounds of review. The decision of the President is sought to be assailed on the ground that it is based on a failure to consider and apply the relevant customary law principles, customs, and traditions of the Nyakunhuwa community in the appointment of the sixth respondent as Chief Nyakunhuwa. This attack requires an interrogation of the customary law principles, customs, and traditions relating to the appointment to the chieftaincy. In support of this averment, the applicants emphatically contend that the sixth respondent does not qualify for appointment to the throne because he is not a descendant of the royal five houses. The contention is that the sixth respondent is of the Baramanza clan, whose descendants do not qualify to be appointed to the Nyakunhuwa throne. In turn, the sixth respondent argues that he qualifies to be appointed to the chieftaincy. The question is whether this court can, on the papers, resolve this factual dispute.




  1. The applicants argue that the appointment of the sixth respondent as Chief Nyakunhuwa is in violation of the long-standing customary principles governing succession to the chieftainship. The applicants contend that Chief Chamagwenjere was the founding ancestor of the Nyakunhuwa clan. This is disputed by the Minister, who says the legitimate founder of the chieftainship is one Svazvi, not Chamagwenjere. It is stated that in the Delineation Report of 1964 Chamagwenjere is cited as Chief Nyakunhuwa the 4th. This position is confirmed by the sixth respondent, who disputes that Chief Chamagwenjere is the founder of the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy. He supports the Minister’s version that the founder is Chief Svazvi. In addition, the applicants contend that the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy is governed by a rotational system of succession among five houses: Mutsvangwa, Mutonhori, Murembwa, and Machingambi. And that the sixth respondent is a descendant of the Baramanza Claim who is not a legitimate descendant of the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy and not entitled to be appointed Chief Nyakunhuwa.


  1. Per contra, the Minister contends that there are three main houses eligible to accede to the Nyakunhuwa Chieftainship, namely the Donono, Muyengwa, and Baramanza houses. The three houses are said to have been founded by a Chief called Svazvi Nyakunhuwa. It is contended that in keeping with the succession custom, the previous Chief Nyakunhuhwa the 15th was from the Donono house, and now it is the turn of the Baramanza house. On his part, the sixth respondent contends that the sole custodians of the right to ascend to the Nyakunhuwa Chieftaincy do not reside within the Mutsvangwa, Mutonhori, Mututuvari, Murembwa and Machingambi families, but in the Donono, Muyengwa and Baramanza houses. Because of the rotational system, now it is the turn of the Baramanza house, of which he is a descendant.


  1. The applicants produced two diagrams depicting what is referred to as a ‘family tree,’ the first showing the sons of Nyakunhuwa, and the second the Svazvi Clan. These family trees exclude the Baramanza clan. It is not mentioned. The sixth respondent also produced his ‘family tree’ showing that he is from the Baramanza house, which house he contends has a legitimate claim to the throne. The sixth respondent’s version is supported by the Minister. Both parties contend that they place reliance on what they refer to as historical documents, oral tradition and the collective memory of the clan.




  1. There is a real and apparent dispute about the Nyakunhuwa genealogy, and which houses qualify, on a rotational basis, to succeed to the throne. This dispute is central to the resolution of this matter. This is so because the first ground of review is that respondents failed to consider and apply the relevant customary law principles, customs, and traditions of the Nyakunhuwa community in the appointment of the sixth respondent as Chief Nyakunhuwa. In other words, the argument is that the sixth respondent does not qualify to ascend to the Nyakunhuwa throne because he is not a descendant of any of the royal houses. On this issue, the versions of the parties are mutually destructive, and leave the court with no ready answer to the resolution of the dispute.


  1. In addition, the applicants contend that the process leading to the appointment of the sixth respondent was procedurally unfair, in that the five royal houses were denied a reasonable opportunity to make representations or to participate meaningfully in the meeting held on 4 December 2020. Per contra, the respondents argue that the appointment of the sixth respondent as Chief Nyakunhuwa was regular, procedural and lawful because it was done on the recommendations of the relevant statutory bodies in accordance with the traditional and customary principles. The Minister contends that the applicants were consulted throughout the entire process. In addition, the sixth respondent denies that there is a royal house that was not heard in the selection process. This issue must also be viewed in the context of the fact that the respondents argue that the only royal houses are Donono, Muyengwa and Baramanza houses. This factual dispute is material and cannot be resolved on the papers.


  1. The applicants contend that after the meeting of 4 December 2020, which declared the sixth respondent as the new Chief Nyakunhuwa, they lodged an appeal with the Masvingo Provincial Assembly of Chiefs. It is argued that the appeal was not acknowledged nor responded to. However, the Minister contents this averment and contends that on 28 February 2022 the Masvingo Provincial Assembly of Chiefs considered the appeal and observed that it had no merit to warrant the redoing of the selection process. In the overall context of this litigation, this factual dispute cannot be resolved on the papers.




  1. It is important to observe that the factual averments for both parties are highly contested; this also manifests itself in the applicants’ answering affidavit, which covers several pages and has 94 paragraphs, some very long. This shows that the factual versions of the parties are seriously contested.


  1. Having considered the competing factual contentions and submissions, I am satisfied that there is a dispute of fact that does not permit this matter to be determined on the papers. There is no avenue which this court can resort to on these voluminous papers to possibly resolve the apparent and substantial factual disputes in motion proceedings. It is impractical.


What should be the fate of this application


  1. This court has discretion as to the future course of application proceedings where there are material disputes of fact which cannot be resolved on the papers. The court can choose to dismiss the application as a mark of its disapproval of the procedure chosen or refer the matter to trial or call for oral evidence in terms of the rules of court. The first course is adopted where the applicant should have realized at the time of launching the application that disputes of fact were bound to arise. See Shereni v Moyo 1989 (2) ZLR 148 (SC) at 150A-B, Masukusa v National Foods Ltd & Anor 1983 () ZLR 232 (HC) at 235 B –C; Mashingaidze v Mashingaidze 1995 (1) ZLR 219 )) at 222B – G; Van Niekerk v Van Nierkerk & Ors 1999 (1) ZLR 421 (S) at 428 D – F; Adbro Investments Co Ltd v Minister of the Interior 1956 (3) SA345 (A) at 350A; Musevenzo v Beji & Anor 2013 (2) ZLR 203 (H).


  1. The applicants knew or should have known that real and substantial disputes of fact will or were likely to arise on the papers, nevertheless resorted to motion proceedings. This conflict is apparent in the applicants’ own papers, since they annex two family trees and further contend that the minutes of the meetings are manipulated and incorrect. In addition, in the founding affidavit, it is averred that “this application arises from a deeply contested and profoundly consequential appointment to the revered traditional office of Chief Nyakunhuwa, a chieftaincy with a storied history and lineage rooted in the leadership of Chief Chamagwenjere, the founding ancestor of the Nyakunhuwa people.” This shows that the applicants knew that their




version regarding the geological lineage was “deeply contested”, notwithstanding proceeded and opted for motion proceedings. Contrary to the applicants’ family trees, the sixth respondent has produced his own family tree. These different family trees show the contested nature of the genealogy of the Nyakunhuwa Clan. On the papers, this court cannot by any stretch of imagination determine which of the family trees is authentic and represents the correct Nyakunhuwa genealogy.


  1. There is a real dispute whether the customary practices and traditions for succession in the chieftainship were observed and followed. The applicants contend that the appointment of the sixth respondent is contrary to the custom, history, law and constitutes a grave breach of the clan’s succession tradition. This is disputed by the Minister and the sixth respondent, who contend that the custom, tradition and the law were followed in the selection and the appointment of Chief Nyakunhuwa. In addition, the applicant’s letter dated 7 and 8 December 2020, shows the highly contested dispute surrounding the Nyakunhuwa genealogy and legitimate houses to the throne. These letters were written before the filing of this application on 31 July 2025.


  1. There is a real dispute regarding the authenticity of the minutes of the meeting held on 4 September 2018. The applicants contend that in this meeting, it was noted that the father of Baramanza, the ancestor of the sixth respondent, was of unknown origin, and this resulted in the house of Baramanza being directed to leave the meeting, which they allegedly did. The applicants aver that the minutes of this meeting are not authentic; they have been manipulated. This is disputed by the Minister and the sixth respondent who contend that the minutes are genuine and authentic and reflect truthfully the events that took place at the meetings. This is not an issue that can be determined solely on the papers in motion proceedings. The applicants knew prior and at the time of filing of this application that such a factual dispute was bound to arise and continued regardless.


  1. In Mashingaidze v Mashingaidze1995 (1) ZLR 219 (H) the court said it was necessary for the courts to discourage the too often recurring practice whereby litigants who know or should know that real and substantial disputes of fact will arise or are likely to arise on the papers,




nevertheless resort to application proceedings on the basis that, at worst, they can count on the court to stand the matter over for trial. The court said unless this practice is curbed, litigants will continue to believe that they have nothing to lose and, indeed, everything to gain tactically by embarking upon motion proceedings, notwithstanding their knowledge or belief at the time of doing so that the respondent will be able to show that genuine and serious disputes of fact exist on the papers. I agree. In casu, it is unthinkable how the applicants thought that this court would be able to resolve the real and substantial disputes on the papers. This is a textbook case where this court should dismiss the application as a mark of its disapproval of the procedure chosen. It is for these reasons that the preliminary objection that there are material disputes of fact which cannot be resolved in motion proceedings must be upheld and the application be dismissed.


  1. In view of the decision I have reached regarding the point in limine that this application is riddled with real and material disputes of fact which cannot be resolved in motion proceedings, no useful purpose will be served by determining the remaining objections.


Costs


  1. The sixth respondent sought costs on an attorney and client. The scale of attorney and client is an extraordinary one which should be reserved for cases where it can be found that a litigant conducted itself in a clear and indubitably vexatious and reprehensible manner. Such an award is exceptional and is intended to be very punitive and indicative of extreme opprobrium. It should only be in relation to conduct that is clearly and extremely scandalous and objectionable that these exceptional costs are awarded. See H J Voster (Pvt) Ltd v Parks and Wildlife Management Authority 2020 (2) ZLR 1075 (H); Railings Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd v Luwo & Ors 2020 (2) ZLR 51 (H). Although the applicants knew or should have known that a material and real dispute of fact was bound to arise, filed this application, nonetheless. In an appropriate case, such conduct could well be taken as amounting to abuse of the process of the court. However, I do not think that the red line has been crossed in this matter to warrant costs on an attorney and client scale.




In the result, it is ordered as follows:


    1. The preliminary objection that there are material disputes of fact which cannot be resolved in motion proceedings is upheld.

    2. The application is dismissed with costs on the ordinary scale.
