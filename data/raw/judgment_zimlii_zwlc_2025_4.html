IN THE LABOUR COURT OF ZIMBABWE CASE NO LC/H/981/25 HARARE, 19 NOVEMBER 2025


In the matter between:-




YELLOWCOB (PVT) LTD t/a SPAR MONTAGU APPELLANT AND

DAPHINE KAKURIRA RESPONDENT




Before the Honourable Kachambwa J



For the Appellant: T. Kabuya



For the Respondent: P. Gomo




KACHAMBWA, J:




THE APPEAL



  1. This is an appeal against the decision of the Designated agent who heard a matter despite accepting that there was no jurisdiction. The appeal is in the following terms:

“i. The Hon Arbitrator erred at law in failing to make a finding that the real issue in casu was in relationship to the Respondent accepting a Fun & Fresh drink without authorisation from the employer especially in light of her position and relationship with Global Horizon as a client.

    1. The Hon Arbitrator erred at law in not making a finding that the Designated officer was properly appointed and when the Respondent had not shown or demonstrated any prejudice suffered by such appointment.

    2. The Arbitrator misdirected himself and erred at law in setting aside the conviction and the penalty imposed by the employer, notwithstanding that the Respondent admitted to receiving a gift (a 1 litre Fun & Fresh) contrary to the company policy prohibiting all employees from receiving gifts from customers unless there is express approval from the CED”.


THE RESPONSE



The Respondent said that the first ground was not clear and concise and must be struck off. There is no irrationality alleged. Documents attached were challenged. Ground No. 2 was opposed in that the appointment was said not to tally with the code and, further, prejudice was said not to be an issue relevant to the appointment. The third ground was opposed on the ground that receiving the drink alone was not an offence in terms of the code and no other evidence was said to have been produced.


THE AWARD APPEALED



The award appealed reads:-


“Having carefully analysed and considered the written submissions by both parties, I hereby order Respondent to reinstate Appellant without loss of salary and benefits from the date of unlawful dismissal.

If reinstatement is no longer tenable,………….

The order should be complied with on or before 30 September 2025”.


It will be noticed that the award does not include the issue of jurisdiction.

However this issue is pronounced in the body of the judgment.

JURISDICTION



The issue of jurisdiction is discussed in the body of the judgment wherein the Arbitrator said that:-

“ I have noted that the Respondent has failed to avail evidence to substantiate appointment in terms of Section 3.3 of the NECCS Code of Conduct. It only made a reference to an Annexure that was not attached to Respondent’s submissions. That being the case I give the Appellant the benefit of the doubt”. (My emphasis)


THE LAW



The law on jurisdiction is very clear. It is sometimes said that jurisdiction is everything. In other words jurisdiction determines whether one has the authority to hear a matter. Once it is decided that there is no jurisdiction then the hearing ends there. One may not go on to decide the matter on the merits, there is no authority to go there.


In the case of Medicines Control Authority of Zimbabwe v (1) Nathan Toronga (2) Nozipho Matshitse (3) Bellington Mudyawabikwa (4) Kumbirai Kajongwe SC 10/201 Gwaunza JA had this to say at page 7 of the cylostyled judgment-

“Jurisdiction in simple terms can be defined as the power or competence of a particular

court or tribunal to hear and determine an issue brought before it. 2A plea of jurisdiction therefore attacks the competence of a court or tribunal to hear and determine the matter. It follows that a court or tribunal that has no jurisdiction for whatever reason, to entertain a matter is not in a position to go beyond the question of its jurisdiction to determine any other issue to do with the dispute in question”. My underlining.


2Page 44-45 Herbstein & Van Winsen, The Civil Practice of the High Court of South Africa, Fifth Edition. Volume 1.






The position of the law has been thoroughly reiterated in many cases. In Gospel of God Church International 1932 v Vendesani Mungweru & Others SC 99/2019 the Supreme Court says:-


“Having pronounced itself on the fact that the applicant had failed to establish the requirement for a temporary interdict, the court a quo ought to have rested its jurisdiction. It ought not to have proceeded any further. In fact, it could not have rightfully proceeded as having found that the appellant had not satisfied the requirements for the relief that it sought, the respondents were entitled to a judgment in their favour. The above position is derived from the settled practice of the courts in this jurisdiction. It is the settled practice when writing a judgment to decide no more than what is absolutely necessary for the resolution of the legal dispute before the court (See Nzara & Others v Kashumba N.O. & Others SC 18/2018 and the authorities cited therein). Thus, for instance, if the court has no jurisdiction, no matter how interesting the legal point being raised by the parties, the settled practice is for the court to merely decline jurisdiction and withhold expressing an opinion on the interesting legal points. Similarly with findings such as in casu, that the application is ill founded, the court has no basis for proceeding any further”.


See also 1. Clawzy Trading (Pvt) Ltd v Jonasi Chitsa & Others SC 92/2024 2.

Chandra Mohan Goyel v Myrammar farming (Pvt) Ltd & Others SC 59/2024.




APPLICATION OF THE FACTS TO THE LAW



The point is admitted that the Arbitrator made the finding that the Designated officer was improperly appointed. Anything done from such an appointment is null and void. Any other further decisions on the issues are also null and void. The course to follow is for a disgruntled party to challenge the decision that the appointment was unprocedural. The handling of the matter by the unprocedurally appointed Designated officer must be set aside and so should subsequent proceedings.

The matter before the Arbitrator was clear that he had to determine whether or not The Designated officer was properly appointed. He did make a decision on it but unfortunately after finding that the Designated officer was unprocedurally appointed he went on to determine other issues and in his award he did not make any award on the appointment of the Designated officer. It is fortunate that there is a clear finding on the issue in the body of the proceedings and it is not in dispute that the finding is there. If the Arbitrator had followed the clear argument he should have proceeded to declare the proceedings by the Designated officer as a nullity as requested by the appellant before him. The Arbitrator failed to make the pronouncement in the award. Nevertheless he went on to make favourable pronouncement for the appellant whereby she was reinstated failing which damages in lieu of reinstatement to be paid. With that in the bag the employee probably saw no reason to fight the jurisdictional issue further.


While there is no application for review of the Arbitrator’s decision the appeal before the court is on both the finding of unprocedural appointment and the reinstatement order. It is a kind of conundrum really. Matters beyond jurisdiction are certainly not to be entertained. The Arbitrator misdirected himself on that.


THE PRAYER



The appellant prayed for the success of the appeal, the setting aside of the Arbitrator’s decision and the substitution with the following:-


“(a) The claim for unlawful dismissal be and is hereby dismissed.

  1. The determination of the employer dated 05 December 2023 be and is hereby upheld.

  2. The Respondent shall pay costs of suit on an attorney and client scale”.

The prayer is certainly informed by the unprocedural findings. The area for contestation and therefore for prayer is the jurisdiction. The prayer is thus misguided and not tenable.




DISPOSITION



In view of the misguided route that the parties have taken and the consequent untenable prayer it is the court’s view that the appeal be struck off the roll so as to give the parties the opportunity to reassess their positions and approach the court appropriately if they so wish. In the circumstances costs shall follow the outcome.


It is accordingly ordered that;



The appeal be and is hereby struck off with costs.
