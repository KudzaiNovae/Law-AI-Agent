Cooper v Bezuidenhout and Another (HCBC 704/25) [2026] ZWBHC 105 (5 August 2026)

Court: Bulawayo High Court. Judgment date: 5 August 2026.

Held: A settlor who divested all rights lacks locus standi; beneficiaries must be joined when relief affects their interests.

Opposed Application

 

 

T. Zhuwarara, for the applicant

MOYO J: This is an application for a declaratory order confirming the removal of 1st respondent as trustee of the Damo Trust registered in 2014 and for an order for the delivery of all trust property, original Deed of Trust for Damo Trust, original Deed of Transfer for stand 503 Acacia Victoria Falls and accounts by 1st respondent to the applicant being the settlor of Damo Trust.

1st respondent raised 2 preliminary objections that of fatal non joinder of the beneficiaries of the Trust and that applicant as a settlor and not a beneficiary of the Trust divested himself of any rights in the Trust and therefore has no locus standi to sue the 1st respondent in the manner he is doing.

Applicant’s counsel argued that there is nothing material in so far as the beneficiaries are concerned and that they have no interest in this matter and that non-joinder or misjoinder is not fatal to court proceedings in terms of the Rules. Applicant argues that a Trust can sue and be sued in its own name and that Rule 32(11) of the High Court Rules provides that joinder is of no consequence and that locus standi must be denoted by a direct and substantial interest in the matter which applicant has. A look at the Trust Deed does not show that the settlor has any powers over the Trust in terms of clause 3 of the Trust Deed, the settlor divests himself of all the rights title and interest. So clearly, the settlor has divested themselves of any rights and interest in the Trust with no qualifications thereto or any reservation of interest just following the provisions of this clause, it leaves the beneficiaries only as the interested parties meaning that they have both locus standi and are the material litigants in this matter.

The general principle of the law of Trusts is that once a Trust has been validly constituted, the settlor divests themselves of any interest in the Trust (and the settlor in this case did so in Clause 3), it therefore follows that the Trust as an entity represented by the Trustees, and the legal owners and the beneficiaries became the persons with the beneficial interest unless the Trust Deed expressly reserves the settlor the right to supervise the Trust, enforce any conditions, remove Trustees or bring legal proceedings or unless the settlor is also a beneficiary. The settlor ordinarily has no personal right to sue the Trustees.

A claim for breach of fiduciary duties generally belongs to the beneficiaries because such duties are primarily owed to them. If the settlor has no beneficial interest and no reserved power by virtue of the Trust Deed then they lack the necessary locus standi to bring the proceedings.

Whilst non-joinder of a party would ordinarily not vitiate court proceedings, it is my considered view that wherein the relief sought as in this case,

a) affects the administration of the Trust.

b) questions the conduct of the trustee/trustees,

c) will result in the removal of the trustee,

d) will alter the exercise of the trustee’s powers,

e) and would otherwise affect the interest of the beneficiaries, then the beneficiaries are persons with a direct and substantial interest in the matter and the proper administration of justice demands that persons with a direct or substantial interest in a matter, be joined in court proceedings so that they have a say in that which affects their interests and so that an outcome that affects their interests is not arbitrarily made without their input.

It is for these reasons that I will uphold the preliminary points and hold that the settlor has no locus standi having divested himself of rights and interests in the Trust without reserving any powers, and that the failure to cite the beneficiaries yet they have a direct and substantial interest in the matter, renders the

application improperly before me because the settlor has no locus standi as he is neither a trustee, a beneficiary nor a holder of any reserved rights or powers in the Trust Deed.

The settlor, without authority, tries to litigate a dispute in which the rightful litigants (the beneficiaries and the Trust itself through the trustee have not complained. The position can be summarized as follows in my view:-

The applicant seeks to vindicate rights that he renounced in clause 3 of the Trust Deed. Having divested himself of rights, without reserving any, he cannot turn around and try to clothe himself with locus standi by alleging breach, of fiduciary duties owed to others. Those duties are enforceable by the persons to whom they are owed in my view and those are the beneficiaries not the applicant.

It is for these reasons that both preliminary points are upheld and the application is accordingly struck off the roll with costs.
