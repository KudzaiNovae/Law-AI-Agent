

REPORT OF THE

PORTFOLIO COMMITTEE ON PUBLIC SERVICE, LABOUR AND SOCIAL WELFARE

ON THE


MINISTRIES OF PUBLIC SERVICE, LABOUR AND SOCIAL WELFARE AND SKILLS AUDIT AND DEVELOPMENT’S 2024 FOURTH QUARTER BUDGET PERFORMANCE REPORTS



PRESENTED IN 2026


(S.C. 63, 2025)
1.0 Introduction
1.1Sections 32 to35 of the Public Finance Management Act (PFMA) compel Ministries to submit monthly and quarterly financial and accompanying reports to their respective Portfolio Committees. In addition, Section 299 of the Constitution mandates Parliament to monitor and oversee expenditure by the State and its agencies to ensure that all revenue is accounted for, all expenditure properly incurred and that limits and conditions on appropriations have been observed. In line with these provisions, the Committee analysed the 2024 Fourth Quarter Budget Performance Reports for the Ministry of Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare (MoPSLSW) and the Ministry of Skills Audit and Development (MoSAD), with technical assistance from the Parliamentary Budget Office. 
Objectives
  To assess whether each Ministry achieved its 2024 fourth-quarter performance targets.
2.2To make recommendations to strengthen execution of mandates and the effective, efficient, economic and transparent use of public resources. 
3.0 Methodology
3.1The Committee reviewed the submitted Fourth Quarter Budget Performance Reports and written analysis prepared by the Parliamentary Budget Office; it also identified areas requiring clarification and made a follow-up with the line Ministries and Treasury. 
3.2The Committee had organised a meeting between the Ministries of Public Service, Labour and Social and Finance, Economic Development and Investment Promotion to discuss the implications of late disbursements and non-release of allocated funds.  The engagement never occurred after writing to the Ministry of Finance, Economic Development and Investment Promotion requesting for a date the Ministry would be available for the meeting.  The delay caused the late tabling of this Report coupled by the late submission of the quarterly budget performance reports especially with the Ministry of Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare. 
4.0 Committee Findings
4.1 Ministry of Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare 
The Ministry did not submit its fourth quarter budget performance report for the 2024 fiscal year within the timelines stipulated by Section 33(2) of the Public Finance Management Act, which requires budget performance reports to be submitted within 60 days after the quarter. The report was submitted on 16 April 2025.
4.2Resource Allocation 
4.2.1The Ministry’s budget was revised to ZWG 3 303 708 730 for the year 2024.  Programme 3 which is Social Welfare was allocated 80% of total budget while Programme 2 (Labour Administration) got 17% and programme 1 (Policy and Administration) was allocated 3% of the total budget. This allocation aligns with the prioritisation of the Ministry’s mandate. 
In terms of economic classification, Social Welfare got 63.09%, Use of Goods &amp; Services (31.52%), Acquisition of Non-Financial Assets, Compensation of Employees, Other Expenses, Constitutional/Statutory Appropriation and Capital Transfers got 5.38%. 
4.2.3The bulk of the resources were allocated towards Programme 3 (Social Welfare) at (80%) while Programme 2 (Labour Administration) and Programme 3 (Policy and Administration) accounted for 17% and 3% respectively. The strategic allocation of resources is aligned with the Ministry’s mandate.  The substantial allocation to Social Welfare indicates the Ministry’s focus on ensuring that all citizens have access to basic necessities and opportunities such as education, food security and health care. 
4.3Human Resources
The Ministry reported that it had 1326 approved posts with 163 (12%) vacancies. This shows a decrease from the 221 vacancies reported in the Third quarter and is commendable. However, the 12% vacancy rate may impact the Ministry’s operational efficiency and ability to fulfil its mandate effectively and the Committee will keep monitoring the filling of vacancies. 
Expenditure Management 

4.4.1The Ministry of Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare’s Vote’s total expenditure by programme in the fourth quarter of 2024 was ZWG 187,395,403. Programme 1 utilised ZWG 22.556 million, slightly above the quarterly target of ZWG 22.424 million, mainly for procuring furniture and equipment for the Minister and Senior Management.  Programme 2 utilised ZWG 24.480 million which 14% of its quarterly target of ZWG 174.163 million. Programme 3 utilised ZWG 140.358 million which is above the quarterly target of ZWG 60.624 million (this was attributed to price changes and El Niño drought impacts requiring additional funding). 
4.4.2The Ministry reported that cumulatively, there was low utilisation of the budget in all the programmes. Programme 1 (Policy and Administration) utilised 63% of the estimated budget, Programme 2 utilised (Labour Administration) utilised only 9% of the estimated budget while Programme 3 (Social Welfare) utilised only 18% of the estimated budget. 
4.4.3The low disbursement rates across all programmes especially for Programme 2 and Programme 3 are of concern. Ensuring compliance with labour standards for labour market governance and promotion of engagement with social partners for effective policy implementation and conducive socio-economic relations may be compromised.  The Ministry’s ability to enhance provision of child care and social protection services to vulnerable groups have been compromised resulting in arrears in most of social protection programmes.
4.5Economic Classification (Cumulative)
4.5.1Compensation of employees utilised ZWG 113 856 780 from an estimated budget of ZWG 77 719 800. The Ministry reported that adequate resources were released to enable payment of salaries.  Goods and Services had a budget allocation of ZWG 1 041 372 068 and cumulative expenditure of ZWG 122 070 355 which is a utilizing rate of 12%. The low utilisation rate on Use of Goods and Services can compromise the smooth running of the Ministry’s operations resulting in disruptions or delays, impacting negatively on service delivery.
4.5.2Social Benefits was allocated ZWG 2 084 416 478 and utilised only 16% which is ZWG 864 570 870. The Ministry reported that expenditure was mainly on the Food Deficit Mitigation Strategy (FDMS). At the close of the year, the Ministry owed ZWG 143 million for transportation of grain as well as accruals on other flagship programmes such as BEAM, children in difficult circumstances, monthly maintenance allowances, health assistance and assistance to older persons. These arrears affected the distribution of grain to the vulnerable and provision of quality education.
4.6Performance Management
4.6.1Despite the low releases on allocated funds, the Ministry reported that cumulatively it had either reached or surpassed, most of its annual targets as follows: 
Programme 2, (Labour Administration)- the Ministry reported that 6 847 inspections were conducted  against a target of 6 500.  The Ministry explained that this was achieved through the adoption of collaborative strategies with other Ministries and Government Departments.  The Labour Officers also partnered with agencies such as the National Social Security Authority and National Employment Councils thereby utilising their transport and logistical resources during the inspections.
Two labour bulletins were produced against a target of 2.  The Ministry explained that although the two bulletins were successfully prepared, financial challenges in producing sufficient hard copies for wider distribution was not achieved.
Two labour market institutions were registered against a target of 2 in the Fourth Quarter as this required minimal financial input.
In line with the Ministry’s mandate to regulate and protect the labour market, the Department of Employment Services and Promotion inspected 213 private employment agencies against an annual target of 185, which is commendable.  The surpassing of the target despite limited financial resources was attributed to verification inspections which were done by Head Office in order to validate the provincial inspections.
The number of career guidance and counselling was 82 978 against a target of 40 000. However, the Blue Book indicated that the 2024 target for Career guidance and counselling was 560.  The Committee requested for an explanation on the same and was informed that  the output was measured in terms of number of beneficiaries reached.
4.6.2 For Programme 3, the Ministry reported that: 
i.The Ministry monitored 243 residential care facilities against an annual target of 204. The increase was driven by an increase in distress calls received.
ii.Community livelihood projects established were 11 against a target of 10.
iii.In terms of Cash Transfers, the Ministry’s target was 348 000 beneficiaries set to receive cash under the Harmonised Social Cash Transfer Programme and Monthly Allowance Programme but only 113 186 beneficiaries were paid under the Emergency Social Cash Transfer Programme.
The Ministry’s target for Food Deficit Mitigation Strategy for vulnerable persons was 6 271 573 against a target of 2 715 717.  
Compliant private voluntary organisations monitored were 497 against a target of 500. 
Survivors of drug and substance abuse rehabilitated were 13 695 against a target of 7 000.  
4.7State of Revenues
4.7.1In the fourth quarter revenue amounting to ZWG 87 006 was received. Cumulatively, the Ministry received most of its revenue from sale of goods and services (ZWG 185 645). The cumulative revenue was ZWG 248 390 which was deposited into the Consolidated Revenue fund. Other sources of funding from United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and International Labour Organisation (ILO) in the fourth quarter reported by the Ministry was as follows:
UNICEF:US$ 51 264 (Employment US$24 750, Programmes US$26 514)
ILO:US$ 27 830 (Programmes).  The total amount from UNICEF and ILO was US$ 79 094.
4.8Issues raised in the Auditor-General (2023) Report
The appropriation account received an unqualified opinion.  Issues raised included understated arrears to hospitals under Assisted Medical Treatment Orders (AMTOs), unauthorised advances from statutory funds; under-constituted Audit Committee, absence of a Disaster Recovery Plan, outdated Basic Education Assistance Module (BEAM) operational manual, large creditors under Health Assistance, lack of a Gender Mainstreaming Strategy, non-compliance with procurement procedures for pauper burials and apparent alterations on comparative schedules. 
4.9Progress made in addressing the Issues raised by the Auditor General 
4.9.1The Ministry reported that the Audit Committee was not properly constituted following the passing of the late E. Sapera and the reassignment of Dr. Madzima to the Ministry of Health at the time of the 2023 Audit.  This issue was addressed during the 2024 financial year. The two vacant positions on the Committee were filled with the appointments of Mrs. Matongera, Director of Monitoring and Evaluation, and Mr. Munesti, former Director of Audit at the Auditor General's office. 
4.9.2The Ministry did not have a disaster recovery plan at the time of the audit, as required by the Public Finance Management Act. The Ministry acknowledged this gap and a disaster recovery plan had been implemented. This plan is reviewed annually, and the first recent review was done in May 2025. The plan is designed to ensure business continuity and mitigate the risks associated with unforeseen disruptions.
4.9.3On the review of the BEAM Programme, the Ministry reported that the Operational Manual for the BEAM programme had not been reviewed as was expected after 5 years. The delay in updating the manual was attributed to a lack of budgetary support and the loss of skilled staff, many of whom had sought opportunities abroad.  However, the Ministry was in the process of reviewing the BEAM (Basic Education Assistance Module) manual, which is scheduled for completion this year. Sensitization and consultative meetings had already been held with key stakeholders, including representatives from the MDAs, local communities, and cooperating partners. These meetings aimed to gather feedback and input, which would inform the revisions of the manual to better align it with current needs and practices. 
4.9.4The Ministry acknowledged that payments to beneficiaries amounting to ZWL$77,470,697,373, were processed using Excel spreadsheets following the unfortunate passing of the IT Specialist who had been managing the programme. To address this, the Ministry partnered with the Office of the President and Cabinet (OPC), which engaged Twenty Third Century Company to develop a web-based system compatible with the SAP platform. Although the development of this system had progressed slower than anticipated, the Ministry was hopeful that the company would deliver before the close of the 2025 financial year.
4.9.5The Committee was informed that the Ministry’s Head Office had an outstanding bill of ZWL$11,314,021,087 related to the Health Assistance Programme at the time of the audit. The billing for health assistance is done by referral hospitals at district and provincial levels.  The Ministry reported that it continued to reconcile claims between national and sub-national offices, ensuring that all bills are submitted and processed for payment. However, the arrears had been accumulating due to the lack of sufficient funding and inadequate releases by Treasury. The allocated budget for health assistance ran out midway through the year, leaving many claims unpaid despite ongoing medical treatments being provided.
4.9.6The Ministry had no approved Gender Mainstreaming Strategy, which had been identified as a critical gap.  On a positive note, the position of Director of Gender was filled and a gender mainstreaming concept note had been developed. The Ministry was working in collaboration with the Ministry of Women’s Affairs, Community, Small and Medium Development to develop a comprehensive gender mainstreaming strategy and the final strategy would be implemented by the end of the 2025 year.
4.9.7On the issue of pauper burials, the Ministry traditionally maintained an approved list of suppliers for pauper burials and due to the nature of the service they were not many.  The approved suppliers for pauper burial services were Kurwidzana, All Planet, KC Funeral, Gold Chunks and Mabvuto Kugwizirana Funeral Services.
4.9.8At the district and sub-national levels, the Ministry selected and awarded contracts based on competitive bidding, with three quotations typically compared to determine the lowest bidder. However, in some cases, only one supplier applied and was selected to avoid delays in body removal from mortuaries, as such delays would be detrimental to the hospital environment.
4.9.9To enhance adherence to the Public Procurement and Disposal Act, all provincial offices now had Procurement Officers. Furthermore, procurement of all services was now conducted through the Electronic Government Procurement System (EGPS), as introduced by the Procurement Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (PRAZ).
5.0COMMITTEE OBSERVATIONS
The Committee made the following key observations:
The Ministry submitted its Fourth Quarter Budget Performance Report after the statutory 60-day timeframe required by the PFMA, which undermines compliance and timely oversight.
5.2While 80% of the Ministry’s budget was allocated to Social Welfare (Programme 3), aligning with its mandate, overall expenditure utilisation was very low, with only 18% of the budget utilised.
5.3Out of 1,326 approved posts, 163 (12%) remained vacant. Although this reflects some improvement from previous years, the vacancy rate still negatively affected service delivery.
5.4In terms of expenditure patterns, Programme 1 slightly overspent due to procurement of furniture and equipment.  Programme 2 utilised only 14% of its quarterly target, indicating underperformance.  Programme 3 overspent during the quarter due to El Niño drought-related interventions.  
5.5Cumulatively funds remained underutilised at 18% and Social Benefits expenditure was heavily underutilised at 16%, leaving arrears on flagship programmes such as the Basic Education Assistance Module (BEAM), food distribution and health assistance.
5.6Several long-standing audit issues remained unresolved. These included unauthorised advances, accumulation of arrears, absence of a gender strategy, use of an outdated BEAM manual, inadequate ICT systems and procurement irregularities. While Audit Committee issues were addressed in 2024, arrears in Health Assistance and weaknesses in the BEAM system remained as major risks.
Committee Recommendations for the Ministry of Public Service
6.1Treasury must ensure timely cash releases for social protection to avoid arrears, especially for Food Distribution Mitigation Strategy logistics and BEAM by end of each quarter. 
6.2Treasury should disburse the ZWG 143 million for clearance of arrears for grain transport and BEAM arrears by 31 December 2025. 
The Ministry of Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare together with the Public Service Commission should fill critical vacancies and provide gender-disaggregated staff data to assess compliance with Section 17 of the Constitution and SDG 5 by 31 December 2025. 
7.0Ministry of Skills Audit and Development’s 2024 Fourth Quarter Budget Report  
The Ministry of Skills Audit Development submitted its Fourth Budget Performance Report on 13 March 2025, which was also outside the statutory time frame.   This Ministry is mandated to transform the skills development ecosystem to drive economic growth, poverty reduction and improve living standards. 
7.1Resource Allocation (2024 Revised)
The Ministry’s budget was revised from the original of ZWG 16,159 million to ZWG 142.972 million for the year 2024. The 2024 revised budget allocation by Programme was as follows:
7.2Programme 1 (Policy and Administration) was allocated 65% of the 2024 budget while Programme 2 (Skills audit and development) was allocated 35% of the 2024 budget. This is an anomaly where more resources are allocated to support services as opposed to the core business of the Ministry. 
7.3Human Resources
The Ministry reported that it had 177 approved posts with 115 (65%) vacancies. The Ministry reported that vacancies in Programme 2 which houses technical staff was affecting the Ministry’s ability to achieve its objectives and it would recruit as soon as the Ministry gets a green light from the Public Service Commission.

7.4.1Programme 1 utilised ZWG 38 029 534 which is 76% of the quarter target of ZWG 50 000 000.  Programme 2 utilised ZWG 4 149 351 which is 17% of the target for the quarter of ZWG 25 000 000. The low percentage utilisation under Programme 2 was of concern because it negatively affected targets for the quarter such as conducting of an informal sector skill need assessment, crafting of scholarships policy and careers guidance policy and promotion of informal apprenticeship programmes.
7.4.2In total, for the two programmes, in the fourth quarter, only 56% of the targeted funds were utilised. The programmes underperformed, reducing the effectiveness of the overall budget. More so, the slow pace of spending hampered timely outputs.  The Ministry reported that unfunded budgeted releases were reversed due to inadequate cash release from Treasury.
7.5Performance Management
7.5.1The Ministry reported that most targets in Programme 1 were met.  The Ministry reported that 15 motor vehicles were procured and delivered to the Ministry.  In Programme 2 it was reported that several outputs were delayed and were still work in progress. Unmet targets included national skills profiles, sectoral skills audits, scholarship policy, informal sector skills needs assessments, reviews of skills development policies across sectors, promotion of informal apprenticeship programmes, among other projects. 
8.0Observations 
The Committee made the following observations:
The Ministry submitted its report on 13 March 2025, which was outside the statutory timelines prescribed by the Public Finance Management Act (PFMA).
A budget allocation anomaly was noted, as 65% of the Ministry’s budget was directed towards Policy and Administration, while only 35% was allocated to Skills Audit and Development, which is the Ministry’s core mandate.
The Ministry continued to experience a high vacancy rate of 65%, particularly in technical staff positions, which had severely constrained its operations.
Programme 1 (Policy and Administration) utilised 76% of its target allocation, Programme 2 (Skills Audit and Development) utilised only 17% of its allocation, undermining core deliverables such as conducting skills audits, implementing scholarship policy, and supporting informal apprenticeship programmes.
Overally, the Ministry managed to utilise only 56% of the quarterly target disbursements.
In terms of performance, the Ministry successfully met most of its administrative targets, such as the procurement of motor vehicles. However, Programme 2 significantly underperformed, with delayed or unmet targets, thereby reducing the overall impact on national skills development.

Committee Recommendations 
9.1In the 2026 budgeting cycle, Treasury should prioritise Programme 2 (Skills Audit &amp; Development) over support services to address the current 65% vs 35% anomaly. 
The Ministry should fast-track recruitment for technical posts and provide gender-disaggregated human resources data in its quarterly budget performance reports. 
10.Conclusion
Despite operating under significant budgetary constraints during  the 2024 financial year, the two Ministries demonstrated exceptional resilience and strategic innovation by surpassing key performance targets across programmes.  However, Treasury should make an effort to disburse allocated funds to enable Ministries to achieve their targets.
