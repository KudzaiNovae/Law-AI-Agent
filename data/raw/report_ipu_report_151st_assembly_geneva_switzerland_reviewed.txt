held in 

GENEVA, SWITZERLAND  


18TH TO 23RD OCTOBER 2025


INTRODUCTION
The 151st Assembly of the Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU) and Related Meetings was convened from 18th to 23rd October 2025 in Geneva, Switzerland. Against a backdrop of 120 ongoing conflicts and 310 million people in need of humanitarian assistance globally as documented by the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) and the United Nations (UN) Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), the 151st Assembly of the IPU and Related Meetings was held under the timely overarching theme: “UPHOLDING HUMANITARIAN NORMS AND SUPPORTING HUMNITARIAN ACTION IN TIMES OF CRISIS”.  
Delegations from 129 Member Parliaments, 5 Associate Members and 36 Observers took part in the work of the 151st Assembly of the IPU Assembly and Related Meetings. These delegations comprised a total of 1 103 delegates including 593 Members of Parliament, 42 Speakers and 40 Deputy Speakers of Parliament. The IPU strongly supports gender parity and youth inclusion initiatives. It was, therefore, befitting that 37.1% of the Members of Parliament in attendance were women while 13.7% were young Parliamentarians. 
The Parliament of Zimbabwe was ably led by Hon. Advocate Jacob Francis Nzwidamilimo Mudenda, Speaker of Parliament. Hon. Mabel Memory Chinomona, President of the Senate was also in attendance. They were accompanied by the following Members of Parliament:

Hon. Jona Nyevera, member of the Forum of Young Parliamentarians and IPU Standing Committee on Peace and International Security; and
Hon. Tawanda Karikoga, member of the IPU Standing Committee on Democracy and Human Rights.

2.0SADC GROUP AND AFRICA GEOPOLITICAL GROUP MEETINGS
2.1In keeping with established tradition at the IPU Assemblies, the SADC Group and the Africa Geo-political Group meetings were convened on the 18th October 2025 to caucus and reach consensus on issues under consideration during the 151st Assembly of the IPU and Related Meetings.
2.2Notably, the Meetings resolved to support the proposal for an Emergency Item for possible inclusion on the Agenda of the 151st IPU Assembly and Related Meetings as proposed by the delegation from South Africa entitled: “GLOBAL CALL FOR PARLIAMENTARY SOLIDARITY AND COORDINATED ACTION ON MADAGASCAR”. While the resolution failed to make it to the Agenda after a vote, it brought to the fore the urgent need to address the unfolding political situation in Madagascar which threatens regional stability. Additionally, it prompted the leadership of the IPU to issue a Leadership Statement on the situation in Madagascar.
2.3Furthermore, the Meetings took note of the report on outcomes of the Executive Committee Meeting. Subsequently the decisions were presented to the Governing Council for approval. 
2.4Ultimately, the Africa Geopolitical Group elected Rt. Hon. Alban Kingsford Sumana Bagbin Speaker of the Parliament of Ghana as its Chairperson for the period 2026.
3.0    KEY OUTCOMES OF THE GOVERNING COUNCIL 
3.1The 216th Session of the Governing Council of the IPU held two sittings during the 151st Assembly of the IPU and Related Meetings. As the decision-making body of the IPU, the Governing Council pronounced themselves on the following key issues:   
In line with the IPU’s thrust towards universal membership of the IPU, the Governing Council approved the membership of Brunei Darussalam as the 182nd member of the Inter-Parliamentary Union.
Concomitantly, the Governing Council endorsed the recommendation to readmit the Parliament of Niger as a Member of the IPU. The decision took into consideration the IPU’s objective of inclusivity and promotion of dialogue as a panacea to global conundrums. Additionally, the readmission presents an opportunity for the IPU to support the ongoing democratic reforms in the country.
The Governing Council took note of the Report of the activities of the President, presented on her behalf by the Secretary General. The report highlighted notable activities undertaken by the President in furtherance of the IPU strategic objectives. 
The Governing Council noted that the IPU Strategy is set to be renewed through an inclusive process that will be spearheaded by the Executive Committee. In line with international standards, there will be an evaluation of the IPU 2022 to 2026 Strategy.
The Governing Council took note of the status update on the selection of a new Secretary General which stated that 58 candidates applied for the position and of these, 20 were shortlisted. In line with the approved recruitment procedure, shortlisted candidates will be invited to the 152nd Assembly of the IPU and Related Meetings for interviews and presentation to the Governing Council before the election. 
The Governing Council took note of the Interim report of the Secretary General which articulated activities undertaken by the IPU which are in line with the 5 strategic objectives.  
With regards to the financial situation of the IPU, the Governing Council was satisfied that the IPU financial status is healthy. In this regard, the Governing Council approved the 2026 budget which is anchored on the IPU strategic objectives. 
4.0THE GENERAL DEBATE
4.1A special segment with keynote speakers was held at the start of the General Debate on the theme of the Assembly entitled “UPHOLDING HUMANITARIAN NORMS AND SUPPORTING HUMNITARIAN ACTION IN TIMES OF CRISIS”.
4.2In his contributory address to the Assembly on the overarching theme, Speaker Mudenda called for urgent Parliamentary diplomacy to enforce humanitarian norms during escalating global conflicts. Speaker Mudenda condemned widespread violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and selective application of humanitarian principles, demanding concrete action to protect 350 million war victims worldwide who have suffered dislocation from their usual habitats.

4.3Speaker Mudenda highlighted the alarming geopolitical tensions that have led to unprecedented global humanitarian crises, marked by food insecurity, displacement and significant loss of life apart from catastrophic infrastructure destruction. 
4.4In this vein, Speaker Mudenda commended the IPU for its timely Assembly theme, emphasizing the need for effective multilateralism as critical scaffolding to guarantee the upholding of humanitarian norms, rooted in the Geneva Conventions and international law, legal frameworks which protect the dignity of individuals during conflicts. To that an extent, Speaker Mudenda stressed the critically of ensuring humanitarian access to victims and safeguarding medical facilities and the safety aid workers.
4.5To contextualise and give impetus to the foregoing, the Speaker referenced the African Union’s Kampala Convention as a model for humanitarian protection that requires political will and decisive Parliamentary action on the other hand, while doing the same within the aegis of the Southern African Development Community (SADC), noting existing frameworks that promote disaster preparedness and coordinated humanitarian responses in the face of adversity.
4.6Addressing the specific humanitarian crises in the Middle East and Eastern Europe, Speaker Mudenda bemoaned the inconsistent application of humanitarian norms and the violation of international humanitarian law, which has been exacerbated by idiosyncratic and selfish geopolitical and economic interests. 
4.7To ameliorate against the conundrum, Speaker Mudenda urged the international community to take action and uphold these humanitarian norms, particularly in regions like the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Sudan, where millions have suffered from war conflicts and famine as well as wanton destruction of infrastructure.
4.8Speaker Mudenda concluded by calling for a united front to defend humanitarian principles, encouraging the IPU and the United Nations to champion these norms globally at the same time invoking a spirit of solidarity and urging all nations and individuals to rise in support of those affected by conflict and natural disasters as undergirded by the ethos of the Roman Catholic Church designating 2025 as a year of the Jubilee of Hope. 
5.0FORUM OF WOMEN PARLIAMENTARIANS 
5.1As per established practice, the Forum of Women Parliamentarians held its 40th Session prior to the 151st Assembly of the IPU on 19th October 2025. In the Forum’s contribution to the work of the 151st Assembly, participants examined the resolution submitted by the Standing Committee on Democracy and Human Rights entitled, “Recognizing and supporting the victims of illegal international adoption and taking measures to prevent this practice”. Participants noted that patriarchal norms, discrimination and socio-economic exclusion created structural inequalities that drove illegal adoptions. 
5.2In her intervention on the topic, Hon. Chinomona highlighted that the intersection of poverty, gender inequality and limited access to justice makes women and girls susceptible to exploitation. In this regard, she called for a gender sensitive approach that protects the rights of women and the girl child. The President of the Senate applauded international instruments such as the Convention on the Rights of the Child and the Hague Convention on protection of children which are critical in establishing global standards that promote the best interests of the child.
5.3The Forum of Women Parliamentarians also held a panel discussion on “Transforming Leadership: Overcoming new challenges to gender equality” The discussions focused on how women navigate and transform leadership amid persistent and emerging gendered barriers to political participation. They shared personal experiences of overcoming obstacles, supporting other women and advancing gender equality. In an inspiring contribution, Hon. Chinomona shared her leadership journey which began when she joined the liberation struggle at a tender age. She emphasised that the road to being elected President of the Senate was marked by challenges but she persevered for the love of her country and her conviction to serve the nation and play part to achieve the aspirations of the people of Zimbabwe.

7.0OUTCOMES OF THE 150TH ASSEMBLY OF THE IPU AND RELATED MEETINGS
7.1The Assembly adopted the Geneva Declaration on “Upholding Humanitarian Norms and Supporting Humanitarian Action in times of Crisis”. 
7.1.1The Declaration underlines that the Geneva Conventions, the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention and other instruments of international humanitarian law (IHL), have saved millions of lives and limited the impact of armed conflicts on citizens when enforced and respected. 
7.1.2Accordingly, the Declaration calls on Parliaments to reinforce and champion humanitarian action by working to ratify IHL treaties, integrate them into national legislation, allocate financial resources to aid agencies, facilitate training of armed forces and foster partnerships across the global humanitarian ecosystem – including the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and relevant UN agencies. 
7.1.3Most importantly, the Declaration demands that Parliaments ought to keep humanitarian considerations at the heart of the decisions they take, especially those related to security.
7.2The Assembly also adopted a resolution submitted by the Standing Committee on Democracy and Human Rights on “Recognising and supporting the victims of illegal international adoption and taking measures to prevent this practice” which strongly condemns illegal inter-country adoptions as violations of children’s rights and urges all states and Parliaments to classify such acts as forms of human trafficking. 
7.3The Assembly further passed an Emergency Item resolution on “Parliamentary action against transnational organised crime, cyber-crime and hybrid threats to democracy and human security” which aims to bolster the global Parliamentary response to growing threats that challenge international peace, democratic governance and the rule of law. 
7.4The Assembly adopted the Motion as presented by the Standing Committee on United Nations Affairs recommending the election of the first woman Secretary General to the position of Secretary General of the United Nations. 
7.4.1In adopting the Motion, the Assembly noted that, since the inception of the United Nations in 1945, nine men have successively been elected or re-elected as its Secretary-General. This gender imbalance is principally attributed to the male-dominated international and cultural practices in which the United Nations has operated. 
7.4.2The Motion acknowledged the positive changes around the world during the 80 years that the United Nations has been in existence, including a growing consensus around gender equality and women’s right to hold leadership positions nationally and internationally. 
7.4.3Accordingly, the principle of gender equality has been entrenched in numerous international agreements, and both the United Nations and the IPU have made it a key driver of their work on social justice development, human rights and democracy. 
7.4.4To that an extent, the motion for the IPU to support the candidature of a female UN Secretary General was endorsed by the Assembly. 
7.5The IPU leadership pronounced itself on the unfolding situation in Madagascar which presents a grave threat to peace and stability in SADC through a strong statement that deplores the loss of life, injuries and destruction of property resulting from the unrest.
7.5.1In this regard, the statement calls for immediate coordinated international action to address the crisis in Madagascar. 
7.5.2Furthermore, the statement calls for calm, restraint and inclusive dialogue.
7.5.3Finally, the statement affirms the IPU’s readiness to work with other international bodies for a peaceful resolution of the crisis and the restoration of constitutional order. 
7.6The IPU launched the following publications during the 151st Assembly of the IPU and Related Meetings: 
Democratic checks, military balances, Parliamentary oversight in an era of rising military expenditure which advocates for stronger Parliamentary oversight amid record highs in military expenditure. 
Toolkit on “Welcoming new Parliamentarians, good practices for on boarding and off boarding Members of Parliamentarians” designed to offer insights and inspiration to Parliamentary administrations globally as they carry out the critical task of on boarding and off boarding first time and re-elected Members. 
Gender Responsive Tool kit entitled An Agenda for Concrete Action which is a call to ambition, setting clear standards for inclusive Assembly. It is another instrument to rise to the challenge of achieving gender equality.
The 152nd IPU Assembly and related Meetings will take place from 15th to 19th April 2026 in Istanbul, hosted by the Grand National Assembly of Türkiye.

8.0RECOMMENDATIONS 
8.1The delegation applauds the Presiding Officers for exercising Parliamentary diplomacy through bilateral engagements on the sidelines of the Assembly. Building on these interfaces, the Portfolio Committee on Foreign Affairs and International Trade should now engage respective Parliaments from Belarus and Turkey with the view of formalizing established relations and exploring possible areas for cooperation for enhanced Parliamentary processes. In the same vein, Parliament should bolster sustained multilateral engagements through participation in statutory and specialized meetings of inter-parliamentary organisations the Parliament is affiliated to. 
8.2The Parliament of Zimbabwe should continue utilizing available resources from the IPU for capacity building of Members and Staff of Parliament in key strategic areas including climate change, gender parity and youth inclusion among others. Members and Officers of Parliament are also encouraged to make use of publications launched by the IPU as well as tool kits in the execution of their mandate. 
8.3With the IPU 2022 – 2026 Strategy drawing to an end, Parliament should actively participate in the IPU’s Strategy renewal process and ensure that Africa’s priorities are reflected in the IPU 2023 – 2031 Strategy. 
8.4Parliament through the SADC Parliamentary Forum should proactively address emerging threats to regional peace and stability through Parliamentary diplomacy and dialogue. 
8.5With regards to the resolution on illegal adoption, Parliament should strengthen anti-illegal adoption legislation by reviewing and aligning national legal frameworks on adoption with international standards to decisively classify and prevent illegal international adoption as a form of human trafficking. This should be spearheaded by the Portfolio Committee on Public Service, Labour and Social Welfare. 
8.6The Declaration on the theme of the Assembly compels Parliament to prioritize to undertake a comprehensive review of all International Humanitarian Law (IHL) treaties and instruments, including the Geneva Conventions and the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention, that Zimbabwe has not yet ratified, and initiate the necessary legislative process for their domestication and ratification, as called for by the Geneva Declaration.
8.7Parliament should prioritize the integration of ratified IHL treaties and humanitarian norms into relevant national legislation to ensure their effective enforceability and to facilitate the prosecution of violations.
 8.8Parliament should advocate for and ensure the allocation of sufficient financial resources within the national budget to support aid agencies, disaster preparedness and coordinated humanitarian responses, both domestically and in support of regional efforts.
8.9The Portfolio Committee on Foreign Affairs and International Trade should engage the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Trade on the motion to elect the female Secretary General of the United Nations. 
 9.0CONCLUSION
