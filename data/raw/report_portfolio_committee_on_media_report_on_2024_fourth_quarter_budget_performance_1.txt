Executive Summary
This report reviews the fourth quarter performance of the Ministry of Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services for the year 2024, highlighting key achievements, challenges, and the Committee recommendations. The Ministry continued to advance government communication, national identity promotion, and media infrastructure development. Notably, radio coverage expanded beyond targets, and outreach programmes effectively engaged communities. However, the Ministry faced significant constraints, including a 17% reduction in budget disbursements and only 65% utilization of available funds, leading to delays in critical projects like the digital terrestrial television migration. Human resource challenges, such as a 14% vacancy rate and shortages in technical staff, alongside unresolved audit concerns and procurement irregularities, further hampered performance. Infrastructure gaps, particularly in rural areas, and slow adoption of modern communication technologies limited outreach and efficiency. The Committee recommends increased and sustained funding, enhanced recruitment and training of technical personnel, investment in digital tools, and stronger monitoring and evaluation frameworks. Addressing these issues is vital for the Ministry to improve operational efficiency, fulfil its mandate, and broaden public access to information effectively.

1.0 Introduction
In accordance with Sections 32, 33, 34, and 35 of the Public Finance Management Act (PFMA), Ministries are mandated to submit monthly and quarterly financial and performance reports to their respective Portfolio Committees. This report presents an analysis of the performance of the Ministry of Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services for the fourth quarter of 2024. The Ministry holds a critical mandate in shaping and disseminating information that promotes Zimbabwe’s national values, identity, and interests both domestically and internationally. This report outlines the Ministry’s key achievements, challenges, and areas requiring strategic attention. Notable progress was recorded in strengthening communication channels, promoting national identity, and upgrading media infrastructure. However, persistent challenges including limited funding and delays in project execution continue to hinder optimal performance. The report concludes with recommendations aimed at enhancing the Ministry’s operational efficiency and impact.

Objectives
2.1 To assess the extent to which the Ministry met its budget performance targets for the fourth
 quarter of 2024.
To provide recommendations aimed at enhancing the Ministry’s capacity to execute its
 mandate effectively, efficiently, economically, and with transparency in the use of public resources.

3.0 Methodology
3.1 The Committee analysed the fourth quarter budget performance report from the Ministry of Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services with assistance from the Parliament Budget Office; and
3.2 The Committee held an oral evidence session with the Ministry of Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services and its parastatals seeking clarity on the Fourth Quarter Budget Performance Report.
4.0 Committee’s Findings
Resource Allocation: Budget Allocation Breakdown

In the fourth quarter of 2024, the Ministry’s budget was distributed across five key economic classifications: Goods and Services (40%) which primarily supported operational activities such as communications, media campaigns, and public outreach. Capital Transfers (31%) were directed to parastatals support and infrastructure development projects within the media and broadcasting sectors. Non-Financial Assets (17%) was used for procurement of digital broadcasting equipment and upgrading media infrastructure. Compensation of employees (8%) however which remained relatively low, indicating staffing constraints that may impact service delivery and grants (4%) were used to provide support to media development initiatives and related stakeholders.
4.2 Expenditure Management
The Ministry was initially allocated ZWG 199.7 million; however, it received only ZWG 165.5 million, representing a 17% shortfall in disbursements. Of this reduced allocation, only 65% was utilized, highlighting challenges in budget execution primarily due to cash flow constraints. Additionally, the Ministry faced significant financial issues, including the reversal of transactions amounting to ZWG 44.8 million, which disrupted procurement processes and delayed service delivery. Furthermore, the accumulation of debts exceeding ZWG 70 million was carried over into the 2025 fiscal year, raising concerns about supplier confidence and the Ministry’s ability to sustain its operations effectively.
4.3 Programme Performance
The Policy and Administration programme utilized 59% of its budget, expending 91.3% of the funds it received. However, it faced several challenges, including arrears in staff allowances, unpaid service providers, and limited capacity to maintain core administrative functions due to underfunding. In contrast, the Information and Publicity programme achieved a higher budget utilization rate of 67%, reflecting relatively stronger performance. Despite this, it remained constrained by insufficient funding. The Committee noted with concern that, purchase of the Public Address System continues to be stalled because of financial limitations, which delayed progress toward national outreach objectives.
The Ministry recorded mixed results across its key performance indicators during the reporting period. Among the notable achievements, national radio coverage expanded to 77%, surpassing the set target of 60% for the quarter under review, while the Zimbabwe Media Index grew by 15%, indicating improvements in media development and accessibility. However, several performance gaps were evident. Television coverage remained at 38%, falling short of the 40% target. Website traffic reached only 1.8 million visits, significantly below the 4.8 million targets, pointing to limited digital engagement or content visibility. Additionally, no Digital Terrestrial Television (DTT) transmitters were installed during the quarter, despite a target of two, resulting in delays to the digital migration process.
4.4 Audit Issues: Although the Ministry received an unqualified audit opinion for the 2023 financial year, several significant audit concerns remain unresolved. These include an unexplained expenditure variance amounting to ZWG 8.1 million and procurement irregularities related to the acquisition of security services, indicating possible non-compliance with regulations.
 Additionally, 7 vehicles paid for in 2020 from Solution Motors were yet to be delivered, raising concerns about contract enforcement and accountability. The Ministry informed the Committee that the Office of the Attorney General had referred the matter for arbitration in terms of the contract of sale. Mr Siyawamwaya, the Director for Solution Motors indicated that he was out of the country until March 2025 so he could not be served with the Arbitration papers for arbitration to commence. The Office of the Attorney General had since requested for another date for Arbitration from the Arbitrator which is yet to be set.

4.5 Non submission of Financial Statements for Audit
Furthermore, some subordinate entities, such as the Broadcasting Authority of Zimbabwe, had not undergone external audits since 2021, which undermines transparency and effective governance. In response the Committee was informed that a roadmap was adopted to ensure that the entity remains compliant in terms of the law by the first quarter of 2026. The strategy involved submitting the draft financial statements for the years 2022 and 2023 to the Office of the Auditor General by June 30, 2025, for a concurrent audit of the two years. The draft financial statements for 2022 were complete, and the 2023 statements were 75% complete. It was anticipated that the 2022 and 2023 financial statements would be audited by September 30, 2025, to facilitate audit of the 2024 financial statements in the year's final quarter.

5.0 Committee Observations
Despite the notable positive developments, the Ministry encountered several challenges:
5.1 The Committee noted that limited budget allocations led to delays in implementing priority projects, especially the digitization programme. This led to digitization project stalling resulting in overruns in time and costs and inability to complete the project within the specified time frame. According to BAZ, the primary reason for the lack of digital terrestrial television (DTT) progress in the year 2024 is non-disbursement of the allocated Treasury funds.
5.2 Key technical departments were affected by understaffing, particularly in engineering and IT support roles.
5.3 The Committee notes with concern that rural areas still lag behind in terms of media access due to delayed construction of transmission towers and power supply issues. This results in some areas remaining marginalised in terms of access to information.
5.4 The Committee noted that, there is slow adaptation to modern communication tools and systems which hindered the Ministry’s efficiency in delivering real-time information to the public.
6.0 Committee Recommendations
The Committee therefore proposes the following recommendations to improve future performance and effectiveness:
6.1 There is need for Treasury to increase budget support and secure consistent financial backing for core programmes like the digitization initiative and rural transmission coverage by 31 December 2025.
6.2 Treasury should leverage on Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs) to fund infrastructure projects, especially in hard-to-reach areas by 31 December 2025.
6.3 That Treasury prioritizes recruitment and training of technical personnel to mitigate skills gaps by 31 December 2025.
6.4 The Ministry of Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services should invest in modern communication systems and analytics tools to boost efficiency and engagement in the spirit of digital innovation by December 2026.
6.5 That the Ministry of Information, Publicity and Broadcasting Services should establish a robust Monitoring and Evaluation (M&amp;E) framework to track the impact of communication strategies and adjust approaches accordingly by December 2025.
Conclusion
The Fourth Quarter (Q4) Budget Performance Report of 2024 showcased the Ministry’s commitment to enhancing government communication and broadening access to information. The expansion of broadcasting services and proactive outreach campaigns are commendable. However, achieving greater impact will require overcoming funding constraints, addressing human resource shortages, and embracing digital transformation. By implementing the above recommendations, the Ministry can enhance its operational efficiency and achieve its mandate more effectively.
