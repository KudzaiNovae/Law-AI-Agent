DUBE-BANDA J:

  1. This is an urgent chamber application. The application is opposed by the first respondent. The second respondent is cited in its official capacity because the implementation of the provisional order sought by the applicant, if granted, will require its services.

  2. In this application, the applicant seeks the following relief: “Terms of the final order sought

That you show cause to this Honourable Court why a final order should not be made in

the following terms:

    1. Execution of the judgment granted under case number HCMSC 151/26 is stayed pending the determination of the application of rescission for default judgment under case number (sic) 236/26.

    2. The 1st respondent shall pay the costs of this application in the event of opposition. Interim relief granted

Pending the determination of this matter, the applicant is granted the following relief:

  1. The 1st respondent and the 2nd respondent be and are hereby restrained from taking any further steps in execution of the judgment granted under case number HCMSC 151/26 pending the confirmation of the final order.

  2. The second respondent shall not remove, sell, dispose of or otherwise deal with any property belonging to the applicant pursuant to the writ of execution issued under case number HCMSC 151/26.

  1. In the event that any property of the applicant has already been attached, such property shall remain under attachment pending the determination of this application but shall not be removed or sold in execution.

Service of provisional order

The applicant’s legal practitioners are given leave to serve this order on the respondents or their legal practitioners.”

Factual background

  1. This application will be better understood against the background that follows. On 7 May 2026, the first respondent obtained a default judgment against the applicant. In terms of the default judgment, the applicant was ordered to pay the first respondent an amount of USD$75 000 or its equivalent in local currency, being the value of chrome ore mined and extracted by the applicant for the first respondent’s benefit in terms of a mining agreement at the rate of USD$25 per tonne. In addition, the applicant was ordered to pay interest at the prescribed rate from the date of summons to the date of final payment and costs of suit.

  2. On 21 May 2026, in case number HCMSC223/26, the applicant filed an application for rescission of judgment, followed by an urgent chamber application for stay of execution in case number HCMSC224/26. In the urgent chamber application, the first respondent raised several preliminary points, which are repeated in this application. At the hearing of the application, the court ordered by consent as follows:

    1. “The preliminary point raised by the first respondent regarding the defectiveness of the certificate of urgency is hereby upheld.

    2. The matter is hereby struck off the roll with costs (on ordinary scale).”

  3. The application for rescission upon which the urgent chamber application was predicated was withdrawn.

  4. On 1 June 2026, the applicant filed yet another application for rescission of judgment in case number HCMSC236/26. This application is pending, and it is on this chamber application that this chamber application is predicated. I

  5. It is against this background that the applicant launched this application seeking the relief mentioned above.

  6. Other than resisting the relief sought on the merits, the first respondent, in his opposing papers, took several preliminary objections which were the subject of argument in this matter. The first respondent raised the following preliminary objections; viz that the application is

fatally defective in that the interim relief is the same as the final relief sought; that the application is not urgent; that the certificate of urgency is fatally defective; that the application is fatally defective in that the applicant procedurally gives the respondents a dies induciae within which to oppose the matter; that the provisional order is fatally defective; and that the resolution is fatally defective. The first respondent urged this court to strike this application off the roll with costs on a punitive scale without a consideration of the merits. I heard argument on the preliminary objections and reserved judgment.

  1. I now consider the preliminary objections.


Preliminary objections


  1. At the commencement of the hearing, Mr Mutero abandoned the preliminary objection of the alleged defectiveness of the resolution. For completeness, I need to put off the way the preliminary objection contending that the provisional order is fatally defective. The context in which this point was taking is the following, in terms of the High Court Rules, 2021, a respondent who intends to file a notice of opposition must use Form No. 24. However, Form No. 26 says should the respondent intend to oppose the confirmation of a provisional order, he will have to file a notice of opposition in Form No. 29B. The applicant used Form No. 26, which refers to Form No. 29B. There is no Form No. 29B in the High Court Rules, 2021. Therefore, to the extent that Form No. 26 refers to Form No. 29B, it has an error. This error might have arisen from the fact that Form No. 29B was the one used in the High Court Rules, 1971. It is important that litigants take note of the fact that there is no Form No. 29B in the High Court Rules, 2021. It is in this context that Mr Mutero, on reflection, did not persist with this preliminary objection. In the circumstances, no further reference shall be made to these two abandoned preliminary objections in this judgment.

The certificate of urgency


  1. It is trite that the certificate of urgency is the sine qua non for the placement of an urgent chamber application before a judge. In Chidau Ors v Shah & Ors 2013 (1) ZLR 260 (S) at 265 B-C _the court said:

“In order for a certificate of urgency to pass the test of validity, it must be clear ex facie the certificate itself that the legal practitioner who signed it actually applied his or her mind to the facts and the circumstances surrounding the dispute.”

  1. The attack against the validity of the certificate of agency is that the author did not apply his mind to the facts. It is contended that if he had perused with care the first urgent chamber application and the rescission of judgment, he would have concluded that the applicant and the deponent to the founding affidavit are dishonest. It is averred that the author merely regurgitates the founding affidavit that the person allegedly served with the summons in the main matter is an independent contract, which is at variance with the contentions made in the first application for rescission of judgment. It is contended that the certificate of urgency is fatally defective, and the matter must be struck off the roll.

  2. In the first application for rescission of judgment in HCMSC 223/26 at paragraph 18 (ii) the deponent to the founding affidavit averred that one Tichafara Musoni, who was served with the summons in the main matter was unknown to the applicant. In case HCMSC 236/26, the current pending application for rescission, on which this application is predicated, it is averred that the summons in the main matter was purportedly served on one of the applicant’s independent contractors. This is the averment that is reproduced in the certificate of urgency. The view I take is that a closer scrutiny of the averments in the first and present applications for rescission of judgment, do not betray an apparent conflict. This is so because an analysis of the averment in the first application in HCMSC 223/23 shows that applicant’s contention is that this person who allegedly received the summons was not unknown to it because he is not its director, officer nor employee, and had no authority to accept service. In the current pending application for rescission, it says the “the summons was purportedly served on one of the applicant’s independent contractors.” Although there is a dichotomy in the wording in the two applications for rescission of judgement in respect of this issue, and for the purposes of this application, I do not accept that there is an apparent variance, which the author of the certificate of urgency should have observed. I think is merely the same point taken from two differently perspectives. It is for these reasons that the preliminary objection attacking the validity of the certificate of urgency cannot succeed, it is accordingly refused.

Urgency


  1. The entitlement of litigants to approach a court on an urgent basis is now trite. This court enjoys a discretion in urgent applications to authorise a departure from the ordinary procedures that are prescribed by its rules. It is usually hesitant to dispense with its ordinary procedures and, when it does, the matter must be so urgent that ordinary procedures would not suffice. See Kuvarega v Registrar-General & Anor 1998 (1) ZLR 188 (H); Ndebele v Ncube & Ors 2021

(2) ZLR 54 (H). In ordinary run of things, court cases must be heard strictly on a first come, first served basis. It is only in exceptionally circumstances that a party should be allowed to jump the queue on the roll and have its matter heard on urgent basis. The onus of showing that the matter is, indeed, urgent rests with the applicant.

  1. In assessing whether an application is urgent, the courts have in the past considered various factors, including, among other; the consequences of the relief not being granted; whether the relief would become irrelevant if not immediately granted, and whether the urgency was self-created. Further, to pass the urgency test, the applicant must show that there is an imminent danger to existing rights and the possibility of irreparable harm. See Ndebele v Ncube & Ors 2021 (2) ZLR 54 (H).

  2. The first respondent contends that the application is not urgent and ought to be struck off the roll of urgent applications. The papers show that the attack on the urgency is rather peripheral. I accept that despite a false start in respect of the first application for rescission and stay of execution, the applicant acted with speed to file this application. I say so because the first application was struck off the roll on 29 May 2026, and on 2 June 2026 this application was filed. This shows that the applicant acted with the required speed in the circumstances to file this application. Turning to the requirement of imminent danger and the possibility of irreparable harm, I accept that this rung of the test has been satisfied. I take this view because the applicant’s property is on the verge on being sold in execution. On the facts of this case the attack on urgency does not stick and falls to fail. In the circumstances, the preliminary objection that the application is not urgent is refused.

  3. The finding that the certificate of urgency is valid and that the matter is passes the test of urgency, is not the end, but the beginning of the inquiry. The court must still engage, and the applicant must still contend with the other preliminary objections taken by the first respondent.

Is the interim relief the same as the final relief sought? If so, what is the effect thereof?


  1. The jurisprudence in this jurisdiction is that a litigant in an urgent chamber application, wherein a provisional order is sought, is not permitted to seek an interim relief that is the same or substantially the same as the final relief sought. This trite position of the law was underscored in the ZIMRA v P (PVT) LTD 2016 (2) ZLR 84 (S), wherein the court stated as follows:

“The nature of the relief sought in the Provisional Order

Finally, it falls upon this Court to examine the nature of the relief sought by P (Pvt) Ltd as against the order actually issued by the court a quo.


The relief sought both in the main and in the interim is substantially the same. This is a practice by litigants that is strictly discouraged by the courts and the law on this point is set out in Kuvarega v Registrar-General & Anor 1998 (1) ZLR 188 (H) wherein CHATIKOBO J held:


“Before concluding this judgment I must deal with a procedural matter which, regrettably, seems to present difficulty to many practitioners………. The practice of seeking interim relief, which is exactly the same as the substantive relief sued for and which has the same effect, defeats the whole object of interim protection. In effect, a litigant who seeks relief in this manner obtains final relief without proving his case. That is so because interim relief is normally granted on the mere showing of a prima facie case. If the interim relief sought is identical to the main relief and has the same substantive effect, it means that the applicant is granted the main relief on proof merely of a prima facie case. This, to my mind, is undesirable, especially where, as here, the applicant will have no interest in the outcome of the case on the return day.”


I fully associate myself with this interpretation of the law. The court a quo should not have related to the application before it in the face of this apparent and fatal defect. What it did was to grant an order which had the effect of a final order on the strength of a prima facie case. The parties before it were not asked to argue on the final order. They argued only on the interim order and the court a quo, regardless, proceeded to grant an order which was neither prayed for nor argued upon. The Zimbabwean legal system remains adversarial and the dispute is between the parties. On this basis as well, the order of the court a quo begs vacation.”


  1. The question that arises is whether the interim relief sought is identical to the main relief and has the same substantive effect? In the final relief sought, the applicant seeks a stay of execution pending the determination of the application for rescission of judgment. In the interim relief, the applicant prays that the respondents be restrained from taking any further steps in execution pending the finalization of this matter. In addition, it seeks that the respondents be ordered not to remove, sell, dispose of or otherwise deal with any property belonging to the applicant pursuant to the writ of execution, and that in the event that any property of the applicant has already been attached, such property shall remain under attachment pending the determination of this application but shall not be removed or sold in

execution. It does not require a rocket scientist to tell that the relief sought in the interim and final is a stay of execution. In other words, the interim and the final relief has the same substantive effect, i.e., a stay of execution. If this provisional order sought is granted, the applicant would have obtained final relief on the strength of a prima facie case. This is impermissible. In Chiwenga v Mubaiwa 2020 (1) ZLR 1360 (S) at 1367 D-E, the court stated that:

“It is settled law that the standard of proof for a provisional order is different from that of a final order. A provisional order is established on a prima facie basis because it is merely a caretaker temporary order pending the final determination of the dispute on the return date. The parties have an opportunity to argue the matter again on the return date.” See Blue Ranges Estates (Pvt) Ltd v Muvuduri & Anor 2009 (1) ZLR 368 (S).


  1. In fact, once the applicant is granted the interim relief it seeks, it would have achieved its main purpose to stay the execution of the default judgment. It will be so secure and comfortable that there would be absolutely no incentive to set down for argument on the return date. See Chikafu v Dodhill (Pvt) Ltd & Ors 2009 (1) ZLR 293 (S). In other words, it will have no reason to set down the matter and risk a discharge of the provisional order, when it will be very secure holding on the provisional order pending the finalisation of the application for rescission of judgment. In ZIMRA v P (PVT) LTD supra, an interim relief that is identical in effect with the final relief sought was classified as an apparent fatal defect. In fact, the court proceeded to say, “the court a quo should not have related to the application before it in the face of this apparent and fatal defect.” It is for these reasons that I agree with Mr Mutero that the application is fatally defective in that the interim relief seeks to provide the same substantive relief as the final relief sought. The application falls to be struck off the roll.


  1. Because of the decision I have reached regarding the preliminary objection that the application is fatally defective in that the interim relief sought provides the same substantive relief as the final relief, I do not think it necessary, and in fact, no useful purpose will be served by determining the remaining objection that the application is fatally defective in that the applicant procedurally gives the respondent a dice indiciae within which to file a notice of opposition.

Costs

  1. The first respondent sought costs on an attorney and client. The scale of attorney and client is an extraordinary one which should be reserved for cases where it can be found that a litigant conducted itself in a clear and indubitably vexatious and reprehensible manner. Such an award is exceptional and is intended to be very punitive and indicative of extreme opprobrium. It should only be in relation to conduct that is clearly and extremely scandalous and objectionable that these exceptional costs are awarded. See H J Voster (Pvt) Ltd v Parks and Wildlife Management Authority 2020 (2) ZLR 1075 (H); Railings Enterprises (Pvt) Ltd v Luwo & Ors 2020 (2) ZLR 51 (H).


  1. In casu, as stated above, the applicant previously filed an application for rescission of judgment and an urgent chamber application for stay of execution. The first respondent filed a notice of opposition and raised substantially the same preliminary objections he took in this matter. The court found that the certificate of urgency was defective and struck the matter off the roll. Having found that the certificate of urgency was fatally defective, the court did not deal with the other preliminary objections. Instead of taking a step back and reflecting on its application in the context of the preliminary objections taken in the first application, which were not determined, the applicant brought an identical application. To be precise, in the first urgent application, the interim relief and the final relief sought were substantially the same as in this application, and it was attacked on the same basis that it was for that reason fatally defective. Instead of familiarising itself with the jurisprudence emanating from case law, the applicant brought the same application seeking an interim relief that is substantially the same as the final relief sought. This is disconcerting. It suggests some element of recklessness. However, I take the view that this is a borderline case between costs on the ordinary scale and costs on an attorney and client scale. I will therefore spare the applicant costs on an attorney and client scale.


In the result, it is ordered as follows:

  1. The preliminary objection that the application is fatally defective in that the interim relief is the same as the final relief sought is upheld.

  2. The application is struck off the roll with costs on the ordinary scale.
